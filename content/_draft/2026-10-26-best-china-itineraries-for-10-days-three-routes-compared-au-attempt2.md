## Best China Itineraries for 10 Days: Three Routes Compared
## Introduction

If you're planning a trip to China, you're in for an incredible adventure. With its rich history, diverse culture, and stunning landscapes, China has something to offer every traveler. In this guide, I'll compare three different 10 - day itineraries to help you plan your dream trip. Whether you're interested in ancient history, modern cities, or natural beauty, there's a route for you.

[Image:Ancient Forbidden City buildings with red walls and yellow roofs, in Beijing, with a clear blue sky in the background]

## Route 1: Beijing, Xi'an, and Shanghai

### Beijing

Beijing, the capital city of China, is a must-visit destination. Start your trip by exploring the Forbidden City, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the former imperial palace of the Ming and Qing dynasties. Next, head to the Tiananmen Square, the largest public square in the world. Don't forget to visit the Great Wall, one of the seven wonders of the world. You can choose to visit the Badaling section, which is the most popular, or the Mutianyu section, which is less crowded.

In the evening, explore the hutongs, the traditional alleyways of Beijing. You can take a rickshaw ride or visit a local tea house to experience the local culture. For food, try Peking duck, a famous Beijing dish. You can find it at many restaurants in the city.

### Xi'an

Xi'an, the ancient capital of China, is another important destination. Start your visit by exploring the Terracotta Army, a collection of life-sized clay sculptures that were buried with Emperor Qin Shi Huang in the 3rd century BC. Next, visit the City Wall of Xi'an, which is one of the oldest and largest city walls in China. You can take a bike ride on the wall or visit one of the watchtowers.

In the evening, explore the Muslim Quarter, a vibrant neighborhood with many restaurants, shops, and mosques. For food, try yangrou paomo, a traditional Xi'an dish made with bread soaked in lamb soup. You can also try other local specialties like roujiamo (Chinese hamburger) and biangbiang noodles.

### Shanghai

Shanghai, the largest city in China, is a modern metropolis with a unique blend of East and West. Start your visit by exploring the Bund, a waterfront area with many historical buildings and landmarks. Next, visit the Oriental Pearl Tower, a modern landmark in Shanghai. You can take an elevator to the top for a panoramic view of the city.

In the evening, explore the Nanjing Road, a shopping street with many department stores, shops, and restaurants. For food, try xiaolongbao, a famous Shanghai dish made with steamed dumplings filled with soup. You can also try other local specialties like Shanghai noodles and shengjianbao (pan-fried dumplings).

### Practical Tips

- **Visa:** Australian passport holders qualify for 144 - hour visa-free transit in China. However, if you plan to stay longer, you'll need to apply for a tourist visa. You can find more information on the visa requirements on the official website of the Chinese embassy in Australia.
- **Transportation:** You can travel between Beijing, Xi'an, and Shanghai by high-speed train or plane. The high-speed train is a convenient and comfortable way to travel, but it can be expensive. You can book your train tickets online or at the train station. If you prefer to fly, you can book your flights online or through a travel agent.
- **Budget:** The cost of traveling in China depends on your travel style and preferences. You can find budget accommodation and food options, but if you want to stay in luxury hotels and eat at expensive restaurants, your budget will be higher. On average, you can expect to spend around AUD 100 - 200 per day on accommodation, food, and transportation.
- **Safety:** China is a safe country to travel in, but like any other country, you should take precautions to ensure your safety. Avoid walking alone at night, especially in unfamiliar areas. Be careful with your belongings and avoid carrying large amounts of cash. If you have any concerns or questions, you can contact the local police or your embassy.

## Route 2: Chengdu, Zhangjiajie, and Guilin

### Chengdu

Chengdu, the capital city of Sichuan province, is known for its delicious food, pandas, and laid-back lifestyle. Start your visit by exploring the Chengdu Panda Base, where you can see pandas up close. Next, visit the Jinli Ancient Street, a traditional street with many restaurants, shops, and cultural attractions.

In the evening, explore the Wuhou Temple, a temple dedicated to the famous Chinese general Zhuge Liang. For food, try Sichuan hotpot, a spicy and flavorful dish that is popular in Chengdu. You can also try other local specialties like mapo tofu and dan dan noodles.

[Image:A beautiful view of Zhangjiajie's Avatar Mountains, with lush greenery and misty clouds]

### Zhangjiajie

Zhangjiajie, a city in Hunan province, is known for its stunning natural beauty. Start your visit by exploring the Zhangjiajie National Forest Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most popular tourist attractions in China. You can take a cable car or hike up to the top of the mountains to see the famous Avatar Mountains, which were the inspiration for the movie Avatar.

In the evening, explore the Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon, a deep canyon with many waterfalls, cliffs, and caves. For food, try local specialties like Zhangjiajie fish and bamboo rice.

### Guilin

Guilin, a city in Guangxi province, is known for its beautiful scenery and rich history. Start your visit by exploring the Li River, a river that runs through the city and is famous for its karst landscapes. You can take a boat ride on the river to see the famous Reed Flute Cave, a cave with many stalactites and stalagmites.

In the evening, explore the Yangshuo, a small town near Guilin that is known for its beautiful scenery and outdoor activities. You can take a bike ride, go hiking, or try rock climbing. For food, try local specialties like Guilin rice noodles and beer fish.
