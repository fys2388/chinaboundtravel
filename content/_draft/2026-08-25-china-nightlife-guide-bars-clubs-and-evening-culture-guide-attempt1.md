## China Nightlife Guide: Bars, Clubs, and Evening Culture

If you're planning a trip to China, get ready for a nightlife experience like no other. China has changed a lot in recent years, and its cities are now bustling with vibrant nightlife scenes that cater to all tastes and preferences. From trendy bars in Shanghai to energetic clubs in Beijing, there's something for everyone. In this guide, I will take you through a top choice places to enjoy China's nightlife, as well as some cultural insights and practical tips to make your evenings unforgettable.

[Image: A city skyline at night, with skyscrapers illuminated in various colors, and a river running through the foreground with boats and bridges.]

### The Allure of China's Nightlife

China's nightlife is a fascinating blend of modernity and tradition. In the big cities, you'll find sleek, contemporary bars and clubs rubbing shoulders with traditional tea houses and street food stalls. It's a unique experience that offers a glimpse into the country's dynamic culture.

One of the most appealing aspects of China's nightlife is the variety of entertainment options available. Whether you're a fan of live music, dancing, or simply relaxing with a drink, there's a venue for you. And if you're looking for something more cultural, you can explore traditional Chinese performances or visit a local夜市 (yè shì), or night market.

Night markets are a great place to soak up the local atmosphere. They are usually bustling with activity, filled with vendors selling all sorts of goods, from delicious street food to handicrafts. You can sample local specialties like烤串 (kǎo chuàn), or grilled skewers, and 煎饼果子 (jiān bǐng guǒ zi), a popular breakfast food that can also be found at night.

In addition to the food, night markets often have live music, street performers, and other forms of entertainment. It's a great way to experience the local culture and interact with the locals.

### Bar Scene in China

China has a thriving bar scene, with cities like Shanghai, Beijing, and Guangzhou leading the way. These cities are home to a wide range of bars, from trendy rooftop bars with stunning views to cozy, hole-in-the-wall establishments with a laid-back atmosphere.

In Shanghai, the Bund area is known for its upscale bars and clubs. Here, you can enjoy a drink while taking in the beautiful view of the Huangpu River and the Pudong skyline. Some of the popular bars in the Bund area include M on the Bund, which offers a sophisticated atmosphere and a wide selection of wines, and The Captain, a trendy bar with a nautical theme.

Xintiandi is another popular area for bars in Shanghai. It offers a more trendy and fashionable scene, with a variety of bars and restaurants to choose from. You can find everything from hip-hop clubs to jazz bars in Xintiandi.

Beijing, on the other hand, has a vibrant bar culture in areas like Sanlitun. This area is known for its nightlife and is home to a mix of local and international bars. Some of the popular bars in Sanlitun include The Tree, a popular expat hangout with a laid-back atmosphere, and VICS, a trendy club with a large dance floor.

When it comes to drinks, China has its own unique offerings. You can try traditional Chinese spirits like baijiu, which is a strong, clear liquor made from grain. Baijiu is a popular drink in China and is often served at formal occasions. It has a distinct flavor and can be an acquired taste for some.

If you prefer something more familiar, there are plenty of international beers, wines, and cocktails available. Many bars in China also offer a variety of non-alcoholic drinks, such as tea, coffee, and soft drinks.

### Clubbing in China

If you're looking for a more energetic nightlife experience, China's clubs are the place to be. Clubs in China offer a variety of music genres, from electronic to hip-hop, and are usually open until late at night.

In Shanghai, some of the popular clubs include Space, which is known for its large dance floor and international DJs, and M2, a trendy club with a VIP section and a great sound system.

In Beijing, clubs like One Third and SIR.TEEN are popular among locals and tourists alike. These clubs offer a high-energy atmosphere and a variety of music to suit different tastes.

When clubbing in China, it's important to note that some clubs may have a dress code. It's best to check the club's website or social media pages before you go to see what the dress code is.

Also, be aware that the price of drinks in clubs can be higher than in bars. It's a good idea to bring some cash with you, as some clubs may not accept credit cards.

### Cultural Insights

When experiencing China's nightlife, it's important to be respectful of the local culture. Here are some cultural insights to keep in mind:

- **Respect for elders**: In Chinese culture, elders are highly respected. When in a bar or club, be polite and respectful to older people.
- **Drinking customs**: In China, it's common to toast each other when drinking. When someone toasts you, it's polite to raise your glass and take a sip.
- **Dress appropriately**: While some clubs may have a dress code, it's always a good idea to dress appropriately. Avoid wearing anything too revealing or offensive.
- **Language**: English is not widely spoken in China, especially in smaller cities. It's a good idea to learn some basic Chinese phrases, such as "hello," "thank you," and "goodbye."

### Practical Tips

Here are some practical tips to help you make the most of your nightlife experience in China:

- **Safety**: China is generally a safe country, but it's always important to take precautions. Avoid walking alone at night in unfamiliar areas and keep your valuables safe.
- **Transportation**: In big cities like Shanghai and Beijing, transportation is usually convenient. You can take a taxi, subway, or bus to get to your destination. However, be aware that traffic can be heavy, especially during peak hours.
- **Currency**: The currency in China is the Chinese yuan (CNY). It's a good idea to exchange some money before you go or use an ATM to withdraw cash.
- **Opening hours**: Bars and clubs in China usually open in the evening and stay open until late at night. However, opening hours can vary depending on the venue.
