## China Visa Requirements for European Travelers

Before you embark on your business trip to China, it's crucial to understand the visa requirements. Schengen visa holders may qualify for 144-hour visa-free transit in China, but this has specific conditions. You need to check the official government websites for the most up-to-date information. The process can be a bit involved, so it's advisable to start early. Make sure you have all the necessary documents, such as a valid passport, a completed visa application form, and any additional requirements like an invitation letter from your business partner in China.

If you're not eligible for the visa-free transit, you'll need to apply for a regular business visa. This usually requires you to provide detailed information about your business trip, including the purpose of your visit, the duration, and the places you'll be staying. It's also a good idea to have a clear itinerary and proof of sufficient funds to cover your expenses during your stay.

## Transportation in China: High-Speed Trains and More

### High-Speed Trains

China's high-speed rail network is extensive and efficient, making it a great option for traveling between cities. To book tickets like a local, you can use either Trip.com or 12306. When using 12306, you'll need to register and create an account. It can be a bit tricky at first, but with a little patience, you'll get the hang of it. Trip.com, on the other hand, is more user-friendly for international travelers and often offers additional services like seat selection and travel insurance.

Understanding train numbers and classes is important. There are different classes of seats available, from economy to first class, each with its own amenities and price range. When you board the train, make sure you know how to find your platform. Look at your ticket or confirmation, which will indicate the platform number and carriage number.

### Other Transportation Options

In addition to high-speed trains, you can also use airplanes, buses, and taxis in China. Domestic flights are often a quick way to reach more distant cities. Buses are a budget-friendly option but may take longer. Taxis are readily available in cities, but make sure the driver uses the meter or agrees on a price before you start your journey to avoid getting overcharged.

## Budgeting for Your China Business Trip

### Accommodation

The cost of accommodation in China can vary greatly depending on the city and the type of hotel you choose. In major cities like Beijing and Shanghai, you can find luxury hotels for a high price, but there are also many mid-range and budget options. Look for hotels in convenient locations near your business meeting venues or public transportation. You can also consider staying in serviced apartments, which can be a more cost-effective option if you're staying for an extended period.

### Dining

Dining out in China can be affordable or expensive, depending on where you eat. Street food is often a great option for a quick and cheap meal. You can find delicious dumplings, noodles, and other local specialties at street vendors. However, if you want to dine at a nicer restaurant, the bill can add up quickly. Make sure to check the menu prices before you order, especially if you're on a tight budget.

### Other Expenses

Don't forget to budget for other expenses like transportation, visa fees, and any business-related expenses such as printing or photocopying. Also, consider the cost of souvenirs or any additional activities you might want to do during your free time.

## Safety Tips for Business Travelers in China

China is generally a safe country for business travelers. However, it's always important to take precautions. When walking on the streets, be aware of your surroundings and keep your valuables safe. Avoid walking alone at night in unfamiliar areas.

When it comes to business meetings, make sure you dress appropriately and follow local customs. Be respectful of your business partners and their culture. It's also a good idea to have a local contact or interpreter who can help you navigate any cultural differences or language barriers.

## Cultural Insights for Business Meetings and Networking

### Business Etiquette

In China, business etiquette is important. When greeting someone, it's common to shake hands and maintain eye contact. Business cards are also widely used, so make sure you have a professional-looking business card with your name, title, and contact information. When presenting your business card, hold it with both hands and make sure the Chinese side is facing the recipient.

During business meetings, be prepared to listen more than you speak. Chinese business culture values respect and hierarchy, so make sure you show deference to your senior colleagues or business partners. It's also a good idea to avoid being too aggressive or pushy in your business negotiations.

### Networking

Networking is an important part of doing business in China. Attend business events, conferences, and trade shows to meet potential business partners. When networking, be friendly, approachable, and show genuine interest in others. It's also a good idea to have a clear elevator pitch about your business and what you're looking for in a business partner.

## Dining in China: A Gastronomic Adventure

### Sichuan Cuisine

Sichuan cuisine is one of the most famous cuisines in China, known for its spicy and flavorful dishes. One of the must-try dishes is Sichuan hotpot. It's a communal meal where you cook a variety of ingredients in a spicy broth. The broth is usually made with chili peppers, Sichuan peppercorns, and other spices, giving it a unique and addictive flavor.

Another popular Sichuan dish is mapo tofu. It's a soft tofu dish cooked in a spicy sauce made with chili bean paste, minced meat, and Sichuan peppercorns. The dish is known for its numbing and spicy flavor.

### Other Cuisines

In addition to Sichuan cuisine, China has many other delicious cuisines to offer. Cantonese cuisine is known for its dim sum, which are small bite-sized dishes usually served in bamboo steamers. Shanghai cuisine is famous for its xiaolongbao, which are steamed dumplings filled with soup. And Beijing cuisine is known for its Peking duck, which is a crispy roasted duck served with thin pancakes, scallions, and a special sauce.
