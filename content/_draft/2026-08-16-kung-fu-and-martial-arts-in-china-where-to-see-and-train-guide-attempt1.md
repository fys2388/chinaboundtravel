## Kung Fu and Martial Arts in China: Where to See and Train

### Introduction

If you're planning a trip to China and have a passion for martial arts, you're in for a treat. China is the birthplace of Kung Fu and many other martial arts styles, offering a wealth of opportunities to witness these ancient arts in action and even participate in training sessions. In this guide, I will take you through a top choice places to see and train Kung Fu and martial arts in China.

### The Cradle of Kung Fu-Henan Province

Henan province is often regarded as the heartland of Kung Fu. The Shaolin Temple, located in Dengfeng, is perhaps the most famous martial arts destination in the world.

#### History and Significance
The Shaolin Temple has a long and storied history, dating back over 1500 years. It is not only a place of religious significance but also the birthplace of Shaolin Kung Fu. The monks at the temple have been practicing and developing martial arts for centuries, and their skills are legendary.

The temple's connection to martial arts can be traced back to its early days. It was during a time when the region was plagued by bandits and unrest. The monks, in an effort to protect themselves and the temple, began to study and develop fighting techniques. Over time, these techniques evolved into what we now know as Shaolin Kung Fu.

The martial arts practiced at the Shaolin Temple are not just about combat. They also incorporate elements of meditation, philosophy, and discipline. The monks believe that through the practice of Kung Fu, one can achieve physical and mental harmony.

#### Watching Performances
There are regular Kung Fu performances at the Shaolin Temple, where you can witness the monks demonstrating their incredible skills in various forms of Kung Fu, including bare-handed combat, weaponry, and acrobatics. These performances are a visual treat and a great way to experience the power and grace of Chinese martial arts.

The performances usually take place in an outdoor arena, surrounded by traditional Chinese architecture. As the monks take the stage, you can feel the energy in the air. They start with slow, fluid movements, demonstrating the grace and precision of their techniques. Then, as the tempo increases, they showcase their strength and speed in intense bouts of combat.

The weaponry demonstrations are particularly impressive. The monks handle swords, staffs, and other traditional weapons with ease, performing complex maneuvers and displays of skill. The acrobatics add an extra layer of excitement, with the monks performing flips, spins, and other gravity-defying moves.

#### Training Opportunities
For those who want to take their passion for martial arts to the next level, there are training programs available at the Shaolin Temple. You can join short-term or long-term training courses, where you will be taught by experienced monks. The training is intense but rewarding, and it offers a unique opportunity to learn Kung Fu in its place of origin.

The training programs are designed to suit different levels of experience. Beginners can start with the basics, learning fundamental stances, punches, and kicks. More advanced students can progress to more complex forms and sparring.

The monks are strict but patient teachers. They emphasize the importance of discipline, respect, and hard work. Training sessions usually take place in the temple's training hall or in the surrounding gardens. The environment is conducive to learning, with the ancient architecture and peaceful surroundings adding to the overall experience.

It's important to note that the training can be physically demanding. Make sure you are in good physical condition before starting a course. Also, be prepared to follow the temple's rules and regulations, which include respecting the monks and the temple's traditions.

### Other Martial Arts Hotspots in China

#### Fujian Province
Fujian is another province with a rich martial arts heritage. It is home to several unique martial arts styles, such as Fujian White Crane and Southern Praying Mantis.

The city of Quanzhou is particularly well-known for its martial arts. There are numerous martial arts schools and clubs in the city, where you can watch demonstrations and even participate in training sessions.

One of the highlights of a visit to Quanzhou is the opportunity to see traditional martial arts performances in the local temples and cultural centers. These performances often feature a combination of martial arts, music, and dance, providing a comprehensive view of the local martial arts culture.

#### Guangdong Province
Guangdong is famous for its Cantonese martial arts, which include styles like Wing Chun and Hung Gar. The provincial capital, Guangzhou, has a long history of martial arts training and competition.

In Guangzhou, you can visit the local martial arts associations and training halls. Here, you can meet local martial artists, watch them train, and even learn a few basic techniques.

The annual Guangzhou Martial Arts Festival is a major event in the martial arts calendar. It attracts martial artists from all over the world, who come to showcase their skills and compete in various categories. Attending this festival is a great way to experience the international nature of Chinese martial arts.

### Training Tips and Considerations

#### Choosing the Right School
When looking for a martial arts school in China, it's important to do your research. Look for schools that have a good reputation and are run by experienced instructors. Read reviews and ask for recommendations from other martial artists or travelers who have trained in China.

#### Training Equipment
Most martial arts schools in China will provide the necessary training equipment, such as punching bags, swords, and staffs. However, if you have your own equipment that you prefer to use, you can bring it with you. Just make sure it meets the school's safety standards.

#### Language
While many martial arts instructors in China can speak some English, it's still a good idea to learn a few basic Chinese phrases related to martial arts. This will help you communicate better with your instructor and fellow students.

#### Cultural Awareness
China has a rich and unique culture, and it's important to be respectful of local customs and traditions when training in martial arts. For example, always bow to your instructor and fellow students as a sign of respect. Also, be aware of the temple's or school's rules regarding dress code and behavior.

From the world-famous Shaolin Temple in Henan to the martial arts hotspots in Fujian and Guangdong, each region offers a unique and unforgettable experience.
