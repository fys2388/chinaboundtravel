## Itinerary 1: Beijing-Xi'an-Shanghai

This itinerary takes you on a journey through three of China's most iconic cities: Beijing, the capital; Xi'an, the ancient capital; and Shanghai, the modern metropolis.

### Beijing: Days 1 - 3

#### Day 1: Arrival and Adjustment-Touch down in Beijing and make your way to your hotel. After a long flight, it's crucial to take a well-deserved rest and let your body adjust to the time difference. As the sun sets, venture out into the neighborhood. The local street food scene is a great way to start your Chinese adventure. Think of it as your first taste of the Middle Kingdom's culinary delights. You might come across savory baozi (steamed buns), spicy jianbing (Chinese crepes), or delicious roujiamo (Chinese hamburgers). It's like a mini-food festival on every corner!
- Pro tip: If you're feeling adventurous, try some of the more unusual street food options like stinky tofu. It may have an acquired taste, but it's a local favorite.

#### Day 2: Imperial Splendor-Today, step back in time as you visit the Forbidden City. This former imperial palace of the Ming and Qing dynasties is a vast and magnificent complex. Wander through the numerous palaces, courtyards, and gardens, and you'll feel like you've entered a different world. The architecture is a marvel, with its red walls, yellow roofs, and intricate carvings. It's like something out of a fairytale! The Forbidden City is not just a historical site; it's a living testament to the grandeur of China's imperial past. As you stroll through its halls, you can almost hear the echoes of the emperors and their retinues. For a more in-depth look at the history and culture of the Forbidden City, check out our article [The History And Culture Of The Great Wall: Beyond The Tourist Trail Guide](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/the-history-and-culture-of-the-great-wall-beyond-the-tourist-trail-guide/).
- In the afternoon, head over to the Temple of Heaven. This UNESCO World Heritage Site is not only a beautiful place but also a symbol of Chinese imperial religion and philosophy. The complex is surrounded by a large park, making it a great place to relax and soak up the atmosphere. The Temple of Heaven was where the emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties would perform sacrifices and pray for good harvests. The unique architecture and the serene environment make it a must-visit.
- As the day comes to an end, treat yourself to a traditional Chinese acrobatics show. These shows are filled with amazing feats of strength, balance, and flexibility. It's like watching a real-life action movie! Chinese acrobatics has a long history and is a form of art that showcases the physical prowess of the performers.

#### Day 3: Great Wall and Ming Tombs-Take a day trip to the Great Wall. The Badaling section is the most popular near Beijing, and for good reason. As you stand on the wall, looking out over the mountains, you'll be in awe of this ancient wonder. It's like standing on a巨龙 (julong, the Chinese word for dragon) that stretches across the land. The Great Wall is not just a structure; it's a symbol of China's resilience and determination throughout history. When you visit, take the time to learn about its construction and the stories behind it. You can find more information in our article [The History And Culture Of The Great Wall: Beyond The Tourist Trail Guide](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/the-history-and-culture-of-the-great-wall-beyond-the-tourist-trail-guide/).
- In the afternoon, visit the Ming Tombs. This is the burial site of 13 emperors of the Ming dynasty. The tombs are set in a beautiful and serene landscape, and they offer a fascinating glimpse into the history and culture of the Ming period. The Ming Tombs are a complex of tombs and memorials that are not only a place of historical significance but also a showcase of the Ming dynasty's architecture and art.

#### Day 4: Arrival and Local Flavors-Take a high-speed train from Beijing to Xi'an. Once you've settled into your hotel, it's time to explore the local cuisine. Xi'an is famous for its delicious food, and you don't want to miss out. Try some roujiamo, which is like a Chinese hamburger with a flavorful meat filling. And don't forget about yangrou paomo, a traditional dish of pita bread soaked in lamb soup. It's like a warm hug in a bowl! #### Day 5: Terracotta Army and City Wall-The Terracotta Army is one of the most famous archaeological discoveries in the world. As you walk through the museum, looking at the rows and rows of life-sized terracotta warriors, you'll be transported back in time to the Qin dynasty. It's like stepping into an ancient army camp! For more in-depth knowledge about the Terracotta Army, check out our article [Xi'an Terracotta Army: History, Discovery, and Insider Tips](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/xian-terracotta-army-history-discovery-and-insider-tips/). - In the afternoon, explore the City Wall of Xi'an. This is the largest ancient city wall in China, and it offers a great way to see the city from a different perspective. You can walk or bike along the wall and enjoy the views. The City Wall of Xi'an is not only a historical landmark but also a popular tourist attraction. It provides a great opportunity to experience the city's past and present.
- In the evening, watch a traditional Chinese dance show. These shows are a great way to experience the culture and art of China. Chinese dance is known for its grace, elegance, and storytelling.

#### Day 6: Huaqing Palace and Shaanxi History Museum-Take a day trip to the Huaqing Palace. This former imperial palace is located at the foot of Mount Li and is known for its beautiful gardens and historical significance. It's like a hidden oasis in the midst of the city. The Huaqing Palace has a long and rich history, and it was a favorite retreat of the emperors. The gardens are filled with beautiful plants, fountains, and palaces that are a sight to behold.
- Spend the afternoon at the Shaanxi History Museum. This is one of the best museums in China, and it houses a vast collection of artifacts from the region's long history. From ancient pottery to precious jewels, there's something for everyone to see. The Shaanxi History Museum is a treasure trove of Chinese history and culture. It provides a comprehensive overview of the region's past through its extensive collection of artifacts.

### Shanghai: Days 7 - 10

#### Day 7: Arrival and the Bund-Take a high-speed train from Xi'an to Shanghai. After checking into your hotel, take a stroll along the Bund. This famous waterfront area is lined with beautiful buildings and offers stunning views of the city. It's like a modern-day version of a European promenade. The Bund is a symbol of Shanghai's modern history and is a popular tourist destination. The buildings along the Bund are a mix of different architectural styles, from colonial to modern.
- As the sun sets, the Bund comes alive with lights. It's a truly magical sight. The illuminated buildings and the reflection in the Huangpu River create a beautiful and romantic atmosphere.

#### Day 8: Oriental Pearl Tower and Yu Garden-Visit the Oriental Pearl Tower, a landmark in Shanghai. This tower offers panoramic views of the city, and it's a great place to get your bearings. The Oriental Pearl Tower is one of the most recognizable landmarks in Shanghai. It provides a great view of the city's skyline and is a popular tourist attraction.
- In the afternoon, explore the Yu Garden. This traditional Chinese garden is a peaceful retreat in the middle of the city. With its pavilions, ponds, and beautiful plants, it's like a little piece of paradise. The Yu Garden is a place of beauty and tranquility. It's a great place to escape the hustle and bustle of the city and enjoy some peace and quiet.
- In the evening, watch a traditional Chinese opera show. Chinese opera is a unique and fascinating art form, and it's a great way to experience the culture of Shanghai. Chinese opera is known for its elaborate costumes, makeup, and singing.

#### Day 9: Day Trip to Zhouzhuang and Zhujiajiao Water Towns-Take a day trip to the Zhouzhuang Water Town. This beautiful ancient town is located about 100 kilometers from Shanghai and is known for its canals, bridges, and traditional architecture. It's like stepping back in time to a bygone era. The Zhouzhuang Water Town is a charming and idyllic place. It's a great place to experience the traditional Chinese way of life.
- Spend the afternoon visiting the Zhujiajiao Water Town. Another gem, Zhujiajiao offers a similar experience with its own unique charm. The Zhujiajiao Water Town is also known for its canals, bridges, and traditional architecture. It's a great place to relax, take in the scenery, and sample some local snacks.
- Both towns are great places to relax, take in the scenery, and sample some local snacks. The local snacks in these water towns are delicious and unique. You can try things like zongzi (sticky rice dumplings), osmanthus-scented cakes, and fish balls.

#### Day 10: Departure-Check out of your hotel and make your way to the airport for your flight home. But before you leave, take a moment to reflect on the amazing journey you've had in China.
