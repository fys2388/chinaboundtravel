## How to Choose Your First City in China: A Decision Guide

### Introduction
China, a land that seems to straddle the line between ancient enigma and modern wonder, is a siren call for travelers from Australia and New Zealand. It's a place where rich cultures, diverse landscapes, and a long and storied history await exploration. However, with its vastness and diversity, picking that first city to explore can be a bit like trying to find the right path in a maze filled with countless alluring options. In this guide, I'll be your Minotaur-slaying hero, helping you navigate through the decision-making process. We'll take into account your interests, travel style, and practical considerations. So, let's embark on this journey to find the perfect starting point for your Chinese adventure!

### Interests: The Compass of Your Chinese Adventure
China is a veritable cornucopia of experiences, each tailored to different interests.

#### History and Culture
If you're the type who gets excited about ancient civilizations and historical landmarks, several cities should be at the forefront of your list.
- **Beijing**: Think of Beijing as a living, breathing museum. The Forbidden City is like a time-capsule, a vast complex of palaces that once housed emperors and their retinues. It's a place where you can almost hear the echoes of ancient footsteps. The Great Wall, that colossal feat of engineering, is not just a wall but a symbol of China's indomitable spirit. And the Temple of Heaven, with its intricate architecture, was where emperors would perform sacrifices and pray for good harvests. It's a place that oozes history and culture. For a more in-depth look at the history and culture of the Great Wall, check out [The History And Culture Of The Great Wall: Beyond The Tourist Trail Guide](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/the-history - and-culture - of-the - great-wall - beyond-the - tourist-trail - guide/).
- **Xi'an**: This city is a treasure trove of historical artifacts. The Terracotta Army, those life-sized clay warriors that were buried with Emperor Qin Shi Huang, are a sight that will leave you in awe. It's like stepping into an ancient army barracks. The ancient city walls are also a remarkable sight, offering a glimpse into the city's fortified past. You can even rent a bike and ride along the top of the walls, taking in the views of the city below. For more on the Terracotta Army, read [Xi'an Terracotta Army: History, Discovery, and Insider Tips](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/xian-terracotta - army-history - discovery-and - insider-tips/).
- **Nanjing**: As the capital of several dynasties, Nanjing has a rich historical tapestry. Its beautiful temples and gardens are a haven of tranquility. The Purple Mountain, with its numerous historical sites, is a must-visit. And the Nanjing Massacre Memorial Hall is a powerful reminder of a dark chapter in China's history.

#### Food and Cuisine
China's cuisine is as diverse as its landscapes.
- **Chengdu**: If you love spicy food, Chengdu is your Mecca. Sichuan cuisine is famous around the world, and dishes like hotpot and mapo tofu are iconic. The hotpot is a communal experience, where you cook a variety of meats, vegetables, and noodles in a simmering pot of spicy broth. It's a feast for the senses. And don't forget to try the local snacks, like dan dan noodles and spicy rabbit. For a comprehensive guide on Sichuan hotpot, including its history and best restaurants, check out [Sichuan Hotpot Guide: History, Best Restaurants, and Cultural Significance](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/sichuan-hotpot - guide-history - best-restaurants - and-cultural - significance/).
- **Guangzhou**: Guangzhou is the birthplace of Cantonese cuisine, which is known for its delicate flavors and fresh ingredients. Dim sum, those bite-sized morsels of deliciousness, are a staple. You can find a variety of dim sum in local tea houses, from steamed dumplings to fried pastries. And the roasted duck is a must-try, with its crispy skin and tender meat.
- **Shanghai**: Shanghai offers a unique blend of local and international cuisine. The local dishes, like xiaolongbao (steamed buns with soup inside), are a favorite among locals and tourists alike. But you can also find a wide range of international restaurants, from Italian to French. And the street food scene is vibrant, with options like scallion pancakes and fried rice cakes.

#### Nature and Scenery
For nature lovers, China has some of the most breathtaking landscapes on earth.
- **Zhangjiajie**: This place is straight out of a fantasy novel. The towering sandstone pillars, which were the inspiration for the floating mountains in the movie Avatar, are a sight to behold. You can take a cable car up to the top and be surrounded by these majestic formations. And there are also beautiful hiking trails where you can get up close and personal with nature. For a complete guide to Zhangjiajie, including its history and best attractions, read [Zhangjiajie Avatar Mountains: Complete Guide to China's Most Spectacular Park](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/zhangjiajie-avatar - mountains-complete - guide-to - chinas-most - spectacular-park/).
- **Huangshan**: Known for its beautiful pine trees and stunning views, Huangshan is a must-visit for nature enthusiasts. The climb up the mountain is challenging but rewarding, with each step offering a new and more spectacular view. And at the top, you can watch the sunrise or sunset over the云海 (sea of clouds), a truly magical experience.
- **Lijiang**: Located in a valley surrounded by mountains, Lijiang is a place of great beauty. The ancient town, with its narrow streets and traditional architecture, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. You can take a boat ride on the river and enjoy the peaceful scenery. And the nearby Jade Dragon Snow Mountain is a majestic backdrop to the town.

#### Modern and Contemporary
China also has cities that are at the forefront of modernity.
- **Shanghai**: Shanghai is like a modern metropolis on steroids. The skyline is dominated by skyscrapers like the Oriental Pearl Tower and the Shanghai Tower. These buildings are not just architectural marvels but also offer great views of the city. The Bund, with its colonial-era buildings, is a popular tourist destination. And the city has a vibrant nightlife, with a variety of bars and clubs. For a deeper look into Shanghai beyond the Bund, check out [Shanghai Beyond the Bund: Hidden Neighborhoods and Local Culture](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/shanghai-beyond - the-bund - hidden-neighborhoods - and-local - culture/).
- **Beijing**: While Beijing is steeped in history, it also has a modern side. The鸟巢 (Bird's Nest) and the水立方 (Water Cube), built for the 2008 Olympics, are symbols of China's modernization. The city also has a thriving art scene, with numerous galleries and museums.
- **Shenzhen**: This city has grown from a small fishing village to a high-tech hub in just a few decades. It's known for its innovative culture and high-tech industry. You can visit the Shenzhen Stock Exchange or the Huawei Headquarters. And the city also has a modern shopping district, with a variety of international brands.

### Travel Style: Tailoring Your Chinese Experience
Your travel style can significantly influence your choice of the first city in China.

#### Solo Travelers
If you're a solo traveler, you'll want a city that's easy to navigate and has plenty of solo-friendly activities.
- **Beijing**: With its extensive public transportation system, including subways and buses, getting around Beijing is a breeze. There are also many hostels where you can meet other travelers. And the city has a lot of solo-friendly attractions, like the Forbidden City and the Great Wall. You can take your time exploring these places at your own pace. For tips on traveling solo in China, check out our article on [Is China Safe for Tourists in 2026? An Honest Safety Assessment](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/2026-05-26-is-china-safe-for-tourists-2026-honest-assessment/).
- **Shanghai**: Shanghai is also a great option for solo travelers. The city has a modern and efficient subway system. There are many cafes and restaurants where you can sit and people-watch. And the Bund is a great place to take a solo stroll and soak in the city's atmosphere.
- **Chengdu**: Chengdu has a laid-back atmosphere that's perfect for solo travelers. The city is known for its friendly people, and you'll find it easy to strike up conversations. There are also many solo-friendly activities, like visiting the pandas at the Chengdu Panda Base or exploring the ancient temples.

#### Couples
For couples, a city with a romantic atmosphere is key.
- **Shanghai**: Shanghai has a certain charm that makes it perfect for couples. You can take a romantic walk along the Bund at night, with the lights of the city reflecting off the river. The French Concession is also a great place to explore, with its tree-lined streets and cafes. And there are many high-end restaurants where you can enjoy a romantic dinner. For a 2 - day deep dive into the Bund and French Concession, read [Shanghai Bund & French Concession  2-Day Deep Dive](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/2026-05-25-shanghai-bund-french-concession-2-day-guide/).
- **Hangzhou**: Known as the "Paradise on Earth," Hangzhou is a romantic destination. West Lake is a beautiful place to take a boat ride or a walk hand in hand. The tea houses around the lake are a great place to relax and enjoy each other's company. And the city has a rich history and culture, which you can explore together. For more on Hangzhou, including its tea culture and the G20 Summit legacy, check out [Hangzhou: West Lake, Tea Culture & the G20 Summit Legacy](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/2026-05-26-hangzhou-west-lake-tea-culture-g20-guide/).
- **Suzhou**: Suzhou is famous for its beautiful gardens, which are a romantic setting. The Humble Administrator's Garden and the Master of the Nets Garden are two of the most popular gardens in Suzhou. You can stroll through the gardens, admire the architecture and the scenery, and create lasting memories.

#### Families
When traveling with family, you need a city with plenty of family-friendly activities.
- **Beijing**: Beijing has a lot to offer families. The Forbidden City and the Great Wall are not only educational but also fun for kids. There are also many museums, like the National Museum of China, which have interactive exhibits for children. And the Beijing Zoo is a great place to see pandas and other animals. - **Shanghai**: Shanghai has a variety of family-friendly attractions. The Shanghai Disney Resort is a must-visit for families with kids. There are also many parks, like Century Park, where you can have a picnic and play. And the city has a number of indoor游乐场s for those rainy days.
- **Guangzhou**: Guangzhou is a family-friendly city with a lot of activities. The Canton Tower offers great views of the city and has an amusement park on top. The陈家祠 (Chen Clan Ancestral Hall) is a great place to learn about Chinese culture. And there are many local markets where you can buy souvenirs and snacks.

#### Groups of Friends
For groups of friends, a city with a vibrant nightlife and entertainment options is ideal.
- **Shanghai**: Shanghai has a nightlife that never sleeps. There are countless bars, clubs, and restaurants to choose from. You can start your night with a drink at a trendy bar in the Bund area and then move on to a club for some dancing. And the city also has a variety of live music venues.
- **Beijing**: Beijing also has a great nightlife scene. The Sanlitun area is known for its bars and clubs. You can find a variety of music genres, from pop to rock to electronic. And there are also many restaurants that offer late-night dining.
- **Chengdu**: Chengdu has a unique nightlife. The local bars often have live music, and you can enjoy a drink while listening to some great tunes. And the city also has a number of夜市 (night markets), where you can sample local snacks and do some shopping.

### Practical Considerations: The Nuts and Bolts of Your Trip
Before you set off for your first city in China, there are some practical matters to attend to.

#### Visa Requirements
Australian passport holders are eligible for 240 - hour visa-free transit in China. However, if you plan to stay longer, you'll need to apply for a tourist visa. It's crucial to check the visa requirements well in advance and start the application process early. You can find detailed information on the official Chinese embassy website. For more in-depth information on visas, check out our article on [Ultimate Guide to China Visa for Tourists 2026](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/2026-06-02-ultimate-guide-to-china-visa-for-tourists/).

#### Transportation
China has an extensive transportation network.
- **High-Speed Trains**: If you want to travel by high-speed train, consider choosing a city that's on the high-speed train network. Cities like Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Xi'an are well-connected. You can book your high-speed train tickets in advance through the official 12306 website or through a travel agency. For tips on booking high-speed train tickets like a local, read our article [China's High-Speed Rail: How to Book Tickets Like a Local (Trip.com & 12306)](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/2026-05-25-china-high-speed-rail-how-to-book-tickets/).
- **Buses**: Buses are a more budget-friendly option for traveling within China. However, they may not be as comfortable or fast as high-speed trains. You can find bus schedules and book tickets at local bus stations or through online platforms.
- **Airplanes**: If you're short on time, flying between cities can be a good option. China has a number of international and domestic airports. You can compare flight prices and book tickets through airline websites or travel agencies.

#### Budget
The cost of living in China can vary depending on the city.
- **Beijing**: Beijing is relatively expensive, especially in the city center. Accommodation, food, and transportation can add up quickly. However, there are also budget options available, like hostels and street food.
- **Shanghai**: Shanghai is also a costly city, but there are ways to save money. You can look for budget hotels in the outskirts and cook your own meals. The city also has a number of free attractions, like the Bund and the Yu Garden.
- **Chengdu**: Chengdu is more affordable compared to Beijing and Shanghai. Accommodation and food are relatively inexpensive. You can enjoy a delicious meal at a local restaurant for a reasonable price.

#### Safety
China is generally a safe country to travel in. However, like any country, there are some precautions you should take.
- **Avoid walking alone at night**: Especially in unfamiliar areas. Stick to well-lit and populated streets.
- **Keep your valuables safe**: Use a money belt or a secure bag to keep your passport, money, and other valuables safe. Be careful in crowded places, as pickpocketing can occur.
- **Follow local laws and customs**: Respect the local culture and traditions. Dress appropriately when visiting temples and other religious sites.

### Popular First Cities in China: A Showcase of Wonders
Based on the above considerations, here are some popular first cities in China for travelers from Australia and New Zealand.

#### Beijing
- **Historical Attractions**: As mentioned earlier, the Forbidden City, the Great Wall, and the Temple of Heaven are must-visits. But there are also other hidden gems, like the Lama Temple, which is a beautiful and peaceful place.
- **Modern Culture**: Beijing has a vibrant modern culture. You can visit art galleries in the 798 Art Zone, which is a former industrial area that has been transformed into an artsy neighborhood. And the Wangfujing Street is a great place to shop, with a variety of local and international brands.
- **Food**: Beijing cuisine is famous for its Peking duck, which is a crispy, roasted duck served with thin pancakes, scallions, and a special sauce. You can also try other local dishes like dumplings and noodles.

#### Shanghai
- **Modern Attractions**: The Oriental Pearl Tower, the Shanghai Tower, and the Bund are the main attractions. But don't miss out on the Shanghai Museum, which has a vast collection of Chinese art and artifacts.
- **Cultural Blend**: Shanghai is a city that has been influenced by both Chinese and Western cultures. You can see this in the architecture, the food, and the people. The French Concession is a great place to explore this blend, with its European-style buildings and cafes.
- **Nightlife**: Shanghai's nightlife is second to none. There are bars, clubs, and restaurants in every corner of the city. You can enjoy a drink at a rooftop bar while taking in the view of the city skyline.

#### Chengdu
- **Food and Culture**: Chengdu is all about food and culture. In addition to the famous Sichuan cuisine, you can also visit the Jinsha Site Museum, which houses artifacts from an ancient civilization. And the Wuhou Temple is a great place to learn about the history of the Three Kingdoms period.
- **Pandas**: The Chengdu Panda Base is a major attraction. You can see pandas up close and learn about their conservation. It's a great place for families and animal lovers. For a guide to the Chengdu Panda Base from a Beijing guy's perspective, check out [Dude, Where's My Panda? A Beijing Guy's Guide to the Chengdu Panda Base](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/2026-05-20-dude-wheres-my-panda-a-beijing-guys-guide-to-the-c/).
- **Relaxed Atmosphere**: Chengdu has a laid-back atmosphere. You can spend your days exploring the city's temples and gardens or just sitting in a tea house and enjoying a cup of tea.

#### Xi'an
- **Historical Treasures**: The Terracotta Army is the main draw, but the ancient city walls and the Shaanxi History Museum are also not to be missed. The museum has a collection of artifacts that date back thousands of years.
- **Local Cuisine**: Xi'an cuisine is known for its hearty dishes, like roujiamo (Chinese hamburger) and yangrou paomo (pita bread soaked in lamb soup). You can find these dishes in local restaurants and street vendors.
- **Cultural Heritage**: Xi'an is a city that takes pride in its cultural heritage. You can see traditional Chinese performances, like acrobatics and puppet shows, in the city's theaters.

#### Guangzhou
- **Canton Tower**: This is one of the most iconic landmarks in Guangzhou. You can take an elevator to the top for a great view of the city.
- **Food and Shopping**: Guangzhou is a food lover's paradise. In addition to Cantonese cuisine, you can also find international food. And the city has a number of shopping malls and markets, where you can buy everything from traditional Chinese handicrafts to modern fashion.
- **Ancient Temples**: The陈家祠 (Chen Clan Ancestral Hall) is a beautiful and well-preserved temple. It's a great place to learn about Chinese architecture and culture.
