Yangshuo is Guilin's cooler, more laid-back cousin. While Guilin is all about the Li River cruise, Yangshuo is about slow travel—bicycling through rice fields, floating on bamboo rafts, and wandering the ancient streets. It's the kind of place where you'll plan to stay for 2 days and end up staying for a week.

Nestled among the karst peaks of the Li River valley, Yangshuo has been a traveler's paradise for decades. What was once a quiet farming county town has become one of China's most popular tourist destinations, yet it still retains its laid-back charm. The combination of stunning scenery, outdoor activities, and a vibrant international community makes Yangshuo unlike anywhere else in China.

## Best Time to Visit Yangshuo

Yangshuo has a subtropical monsoon climate with mild winters and hot, humid summers:

- **September to November (Autumn):** **Best time.** Cool weather (15-25°C/59-77°F), clear skies, and the rice fields turn golden in October. Perfect for cycling and outdoor activities.
- **March to May (Spring):** Good time. Mild temperatures (15-25°C/59-77°F), blooming flowers, and misty mountains. April and May can be rainy.
- **June to August (Summer):** Hot (28-35°C/82-95°F) and humid with frequent rain. The landscape is lush and green, and the rivers are full. Thunderstorms are common. Best for early morning activities before the heat.
- **December to February (Winter):** Cold (5-12°C/41-54°F) and damp, but less crowded. Misty mountains have a mystical, ink-painting feel. Bamboo rafting may be suspended on cold days. Hotel prices are lowest.

**Avoid:** Chinese New Year (late January/early February), the first week of October (National Day holiday), and mid-June to mid-July (plum rain season with constant drizzle).

## Getting There & Around

### From Guilin

- **Bus:** From Guilin South Bus Station (桂林汽车南站). ¥25-30, 1.5 hours. Buses run every 15-30 minutes from 7 AM to 9 PM.
- **Li River Cruise:** The scenic 4-5 hour cruise from Guilin to Yangshuo. ¥215-360 depending on class. Arrives in Yangshuo around 2-3 PM.
- **High-Speed Train:** Guilin North/West to Yangshuo Station. ¥20-35, 30 minutes. Then take a bus or taxi to Yangshuo town (¥20-30, 30 minutes).
- **Taxi/Didi:** ¥150-200, 1-1.5 hours.

### From Guangzhou

- **High-Speed Train:** Guangzhou South to Yangshuo Station. ¥175-250, 2-2.5 hours. Then bus/taxi to town.

### Within Yangshuo

- **Bicycle:** The best way to explore. Rent one for ¥10-20/day from any hostel or shop. Mountain bikes and city bikes available.
- **Electric Bike:** For longer distances or if you're not up for pedaling. ¥30-50/day. Great for exploring the countryside without breaking a sweat.
- **Scooter/Motorcycle:** ¥60-100/day. Requires an international driver's license (though many places rent without checking). Be careful—traffic can be chaotic.
- **Bus:** Local buses connect Yangshuo town to nearby villages and attractions. ¥2-10.
- **Taxi/Didi:** Available in town, but less common in rural areas. ¥10-30 for short trips.
- **Boat/Bamboo Raft:** The best way to see the rivers. Bamboo rafts on the Yulong River and Li River.

## Must-See Sights

### Yulong River (遇龙河)

Forget the crowded Li River—this is the real gem. The Yulong River (also called the "Little Li River") winds 43km through the most beautiful karst countryside in Yangshuo. Float down on a traditional bamboo raft (actually motorized PVC boats that look like bamboo), gliding past karst peaks, green rice fields, ancient villages, and old stone bridges.

- **Bamboo Raft Tickets:** ¥160-240 per raft (2 people), depending on the section. The most popular section is from Jinlong Bridge (金龙桥) to Jiuxian Village (旧县村), ~2 hours.
- **When to go:** Early morning (8-10 AM) for misty views and fewer crowds, or late afternoon (3-5 PM) for golden light. Avoid midday when the sun is harsh.
- **What to expect:** The raft goes over small weirs (dams) that create gentle rapids—you may get a little wet. The raft guide poles the boat and may point out landmarks.
- **Pro tips:**
  - Hire a local raft guide at the starting point. They'll point out hidden caves and tell you stories about the area.
  - Bring a waterproof bag for your phone and camera.
  - Wear sunscreen and a hat—there's no shade on the raft.
  - Combine with a cycling trip: take the raft down, then cycle back along the river.

### West Street (西街)

Yangshuo's main tourist street, 517m long, but it's worth a wander. Lined with cafes, bars, shops, and street performers, it comes alive at night. Known as "Foreigners' Street" because of the high number of international travelers and expats who live here.

- **When to go:** Evening (7-11 PM) for the best atmosphere. The street is pedestrian-only after 6 PM.
- **What to do:**
  - Browse the shops selling souvenirs, clothing, art, and local crafts.
  - Try the street food—barbecue skewers, fruit, ice cream, and local snacks.
  - Stop at a cafe or bar for a drink and people-watching.
  - Watch street performers—musicians, magicians, and artists.
- **Pro tips:**
  - West Street is touristy and prices are higher than elsewhere. Bargain for souvenirs.
  - The side alleys have better, cheaper restaurants and bars.
  - If you're staying near West Street, be prepared for noise until late at night.

### Moon Hill (月亮山)

A famous karst peak with a natural arch that looks like a crescent moon. The arch is 50m wide and the hill is 380m high. Climb the 800+ steps to the top for panoramic views of the surrounding countryside.

- **Tickets:** ¥15 (hill only) or ¥60 (hill + cave + warm spring).
- **When to go:** Early morning (before 9 AM) to avoid crowds and the midday heat. Late afternoon for golden light.
- **How to get there:** 8km south of Yangshuo town. Cycle (30-40 minutes), take an electric bike, or bus/taxi (¥20-30).
- **What to expect:** The climb takes 30-45 minutes. The path is steep but well-maintained. At the top, you can walk through the natural arch and enjoy 360° views of the karst peaks and rice fields.
- **Pro tips:**
  - Bring water—there are no shops on the hill.
  - Wear good walking shoes—the steps can be slippery.
  - Combine with a visit to the nearby Butterfly Cave (蝴蝶泉) or a cycling trip through the Ten-Mile Gallery.

### Ten-Mile Gallery (十里画廊)

A 10-mile (actually ~5km) scenic road between Yangshuo and Moon Hill. Rent a bike and cycle through this "gallery" of karst mountains, stopping at viewpoints, caves, and villages along the way.

- **Tickets:** Free to cycle the road. Individual attractions (caves, parks) charge separate fees.
- **When to go:** Early morning or late afternoon to avoid the heat.
- **Highlights along the way:**
  - **Peach Blossom Land (世外桃源):** A scenic park with lakes, villages, and karst peaks. ¥60.
  - **Butterfly Spring (蝴蝶泉):** A cave with butterfly-shaped stalactites and a view of the "Ten-Mile Gallery." ¥45.
  - **Big Banyan Tree (大榕树):** A 1,400-year-old banyan tree made famous by the movie "The Liu Sanjie Story." ¥20.
  - **Moon Hill:** At the end of the gallery.
- **Pro tip:** Rent a bike and take your time—stop at villages, talk to locals, and enjoy the scenery. This is one of the best cycling routes in China.

### Impression Liu Sanjie (印象刘三姐)

A spectacular outdoor light show directed by Zhang Yimou (famous for "Hero" and the Beijing Olympics opening ceremony). The performance takes place on the Li River with 12 karst mountains as the natural backdrop. Over 600 performers (mostly local villagers) act out the legend of Liu Sanjie, a legendary folk singer.

- **Tickets:** ¥238-680 depending on seat category (regular, VIP, President's seat).
- **When:** Nightly at 7:30 PM and 9:30 PM (peak season may add shows). 70 minutes.
- **Where:** On the Li River, 10 minutes by taxi from West Street.
- **Pro tips:**
  - Book tickets in advance, especially during peak season. You can book through your hotel, a travel agency, or online.
  - The "B" category seats (second tier) offer the best value—you're high enough to see the whole stage.
  - Bring a jacket—it can get cool by the river at night.
  - The show is entirely in Chinese, but the visuals are stunning regardless of language.

### Xingping Ancient Town (兴坪古镇)

A 40-minute drive from Yangshuo (or 30 minutes by bamboo raft down the Li River). This ancient town on the Li River is the setting for the scenery on the ¥20 banknote. Much quieter and less touristy than Yangshuo.

- **Tickets:** Free to enter the town.
- **What to do:**
  - Walk the ancient streets with Ming and Qing dynasty buildings.
  - Take photos of the ¥20 banknote scenery from the riverbank.
  - Visit the old fishing harbor and see cormorant fishermen.
  - Take a bamboo raft on the Li River (the most scenic section).
  - Hike to Laozhai Mountain (老寨山) for panoramic views (free, but steep—1.5 hours round trip).
- **Pro tip:** Xingping is much quieter than Yangshuo. Great for a half-day trip or an overnight stay. The sunsets over the Li River here are spectacular.

### Fuli Ancient Town (福利古镇)

A 30-minute drive east of Yangshuo. An ancient town famous for its traditional Chinese fan painting (画扇). The town has a 1,000-year history and is less touristy than Yangshuo.

- **Tickets:** Free.
- **What to do:**
  - Visit the fan painting workshops and watch artists at work.
  - Walk the ancient streets and visit the old wharf.
  - See the Three Immortals Temple (三仙寺) on the hill above town.
  - Cycle through the surrounding countryside.
- **Pro tip:** Fuli is a great place to buy authentic Chinese fan paintings directly from the artists at reasonable prices.

## Bicycle Routes

Yangshuo is the bicycle capital of China. The flat terrain, scenic roads, and quiet country lanes make it perfect for cycling. Here are the best routes:

### Route 1: Yulong River Loop (Half Day, 20km)
- Start: Yangshuo town
- Route: Yangshuo → Yulong River → Jinlong Bridge → Jiuxian Village → Yulong River bridge → back to Yangshuo
- Highlights: Yulong River views, ancient villages, old stone bridges, rice fields
- Difficulty: Easy, flat terrain
- Time: 2-3 hours

### Route 2: Ten-Mile Gallery (Half Day, 15km)
- Start: Yangshuo town
- Route: Yangshuo → Ten-Mile Gallery → Big Banyan Tree → Moon Hill → back to Yangshuo
- Highlights: Karst peaks, caves, Big Banyan Tree, Moon Hill climb
- Difficulty: Easy, flat terrain
- Time: 3-4 hours (including stops)

### Route 3: Countryside Loop to Putao (Full Day, 40km)
- Start: Yangshuo town
- Route: Yangshuo → Baisha → Putao → Yangti → back to Yangshuo
- Highlights: Remote villages, less touristy areas, authentic rural life, Li River views at Yangti
- Difficulty: Moderate, some hills
- Time: 5-6 hours

### Route 4: Yulong River + Bamboo Raft Combo (Full Day)
- Morning: Cycle from Yangshuo to Jinlong Bridge (10km, 45 min)
- Midday: Bamboo raft from Jinlong Bridge to Jiuxian Village (2 hours)
- Afternoon: Cycle from Jiuxian Village back to Yangshuo (10km, 45 min), with stops at villages and viewpoints
- This is the classic Yangshuo experience—highly recommended!

**Cycling Tips:**
- Rent a bike with a basket for your water, camera, and snacks.
- Bring plenty of water—there are few shops in the countryside.
- Wear sunscreen and a hat.
- Follow the road rules—cyclists share the road with cars, buses, and scooters.
- Don't be afraid to get lost—some of the best experiences are in the small villages off the main roads.
- Ask locals for directions—they're friendly and helpful.

## Where to Eat

Yangshuo has a mix of local and international cuisine, thanks to the large expat community. You can find everything from authentic Guangxi dishes to pizza, burgers, and craft beer.

### Local Specialties

- **Beer Fish (啤酒鱼):** Yangshuo's most famous dish. Fresh fish from the Yulong/Li River cooked with local beer, chili peppers, tomatoes, garlic, and ginger. The beer tenderizes the fish and adds a unique flavor. ¥60-120 per fish.
  - **Where:** Any restaurant along West Street or the river. Try "Master Huang Beer Fish" (黄师傅啤酒鱼) or "Yangshuo Beer Fish Master" (阳朔啤酒鱼大师).
- **Guilin Rice Noodles (桂林米粉):** Same great noodles as Guilin, but with Yangshuo's own twist. Fresh rice noodles in broth with toppings. ¥8-15.
  - **Where:** Any local noodle shop, especially for breakfast.
- **Oil Tea (油茶):** A bitter, savory tea made by pounding tea leaves with ginger, garlic, and peanuts. Served with puffed rice, peanuts, and snacks. Popular with the local Zhuang and Yao minorities. ¥10-20.
- **Stuffed Li River Snails (漓江田螺):** Large snails stuffed with minced pork, mint, and spices. A local specialty. ¥20-30.
- **Bamboo Rice (竹筒饭):** Rice cooked in a bamboo tube with meat, mushrooms, and vegetables. The bamboo adds a unique aroma. ¥15-25.
- **Osmanthus Cake (桂花糕):** A sweet snack made with osmanthus flowers, glutinous rice, and sugar. ¥5-10.

### International Food

Yangshuo has excellent Western options, thanks to the expat community:
- **Pizza:** "The Pizza Factory" or "Pizza Pazza" for authentic Italian pizza.
- **Burgers:** "Burger Club" or "The Brew" for craft burgers.
- **Coffee:** "Yangshuo Coffee" or "The Bookworm" for good coffee and workspace.
- **Craft Beer:** "Yangshuo Brewery" or "The Brew" for local craft beer.
- **Vegetarian/Vegan:** "Lazy Cat's Kitchen" or "Green Lotus" for vegetarian options.

### Restaurants

- **Lazy Cat's Kitchen (懒猫厨房):** Popular with travelers, serving both Chinese and Western food. Great for breakfast and coffee. ¥50-100 per person.
- **The Brew:** Craft beer pub with good burgers and pizza. Popular with expats. ¥80-150 per person.
- **Master Huang Beer Fish (黄师傅啤酒鱼):** Famous for beer fish. Local favorite. ¥80-120 per person.
- **Shiwai Taoyuan Restaurant (世外桃源餐厅):** In a beautiful garden setting, serving traditional Yangshuo cuisine. ¥80-150 per person.
- **West Street Food Stalls:** For cheap, quick eats—barbecue skewers (¥2-5 each), fruit (¥10-20), crepes (¥15-25), and ice cream.

## Where to Stay

Yangshuo has accommodation for every budget, from dorm beds to luxury resorts.

### West Street Area
- **Best for:** First-time visitors, nightlife, convenience.
- **Price range:** ¥100-800/night.
- **Pros:** Walk to restaurants, bars, shops, and the river. Easy to meet other travelers.
- **Cons:** Noisy at night (bars play music until late). More touristy.

### Yulong River Area
- **Best for:** Couples, relaxation, scenery, longer stays.
- **Price range:** ¥200-1500/night.
- **Pros:** Quiet, scenic, surrounded by karst peaks and rice fields. Great for cycling and rafting.
- **Cons:** 3-5km from West Street. Need a bike/electric bike to get to town. Fewer restaurant options.

### Xingping Ancient Town
- **Best for:** Authentic experience, photography, quiet retreat.
- **Price range:** ¥150-800/night.
- **Pros:** Much quieter than Yangshuo. Beautiful Li River views. Close to the ¥20 banknote scenery.
- **Cons:** 40 minutes from Yangshuo town. Fewer amenities and restaurant options.

### Fuli Ancient Town
- **Best for:** Art lovers, off-the-beaten-path.
- **Price range:** ¥100-500/night.
- **Pros:** Very authentic, almost no tourists. Famous for fan painting. Cheap.
- **Cons:** 30 minutes from Yangshuo. Very limited amenities.

### Budget Options
- **Yangshuo Showbiz Hostel:** Near West Street, popular with backpackers, social atmosphere. ¥50-100/night for a dorm bed.
- **The Bamboo Leaf Hostel:** Quiet location near the river, clean and friendly. ¥60-120/night.
- **Yangshuo Mountain Retreat:** In the Yulong River area, beautiful views, mid-range. ¥300-600/night.

### Luxury Options
- **Yangshuo Sugar House:** A luxury boutique hotel in a converted sugar mill, designed by a famous architect. ¥1500-3000/night.
- **Banyan Tree Yangshuo:** Luxury resort with villas, spa, and river views. ¥2000-5000/night.
- **Alila Yangshuo:** Luxury resort in a converted sugar mill, with stunning architecture. ¥1500-3500/night.

## Practical Tips

### Money & Payments
- Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted everywhere in Yangshuo, including most restaurants, shops, and ticket counters. West Street has many ATMs.
- Carry ¥200-500 cash for small vendors, villages, and emergencies. Some rural shops may not accept mobile payment.
- ATMs are available in town, but may be limited in rural areas.

### Internet & VPN
- Google, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, YouTube are blocked.
- **Set up a VPN before arrival.** ExpressVPN, Astrill, and NordVPN work well.
- 4G/5G coverage is good in town and along the main roads, but may be spotty in remote villages and mountainous areas.
- Many cafes, hostels, and hotels offer free WiFi.

### Language
- English is widely spoken in West Street and tourist areas, thanks to the large expat community. Many restaurant menus have English translations.
- In rural areas and villages, English is less common. Download Pleco (Chinese dictionary) and Google Translate (offline pack) for communication.
- The local dialect is Southwest Mandarin, similar to Sichuan dialect, but most people speak standard Mandarin.

### Safety
- Yangshuo is very safe. Violent crime is rare.
- Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas (West Street, bus stations, tourist sites).
- Be careful when cycling—share the road with cars, buses, and scooters. Wear a helmet if available.
- Be careful when hiking—some trails can be slippery, especially in the rain. Moon Hill and Laozhai Mountain have steep sections.
- Use licensed tour operators and official ticket counters. Avoid "free tours" that lead to shopping scams.
- The rivers can have strong currents—swimming is not recommended. Wear life jackets on bamboo rafts (provided).
- Drink responsibly in West Street—some bars may overcharge tourists. Check prices before ordering.

### Health
- Yangshuo is humid—stay hydrated and use mosquito repellent, especially near the river and in rural areas.
- The food is generally safe—stick to busy restaurants with high turnover. Street food is generally safe if it's cooked fresh in front of you.
- Drink bottled or boiled water only.
- Bring sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses—the sun can be strong even on cloudy days.
- If cycling or hiking, bring proper shoes and plenty of water.
- Pharmacies are available in town. For serious medical issues, go to Guilin (1.5 hours away) for better hospitals.

### Tipping
- Tipping is not customary. No need to tip at restaurants, for taxis, or for guides.
- Some high-end hotels and tour guides may appreciate a small tip, but it's not expected.
- Bamboo raft guides don't expect tips, but a small tip (¥10-20) is appreciated for good service.

### What to Pack
- Comfortable walking/cycling shoes (you'll be cycling and walking a lot)
- Light, breathable clothing (humid climate)
- Rain jacket or umbrella (frequent rain, especially in spring and summer)
- Sunscreen, hat, and sunglasses
- Portable power bank (essential for navigation, photos, and payments)
- Universal adapter (Type A, C, I, 220V)
- Mosquito repellent
- Toilet paper (many public restrooms don't provide it)
- Hand sanitizer
- Swimsuit (if your hotel has a pool, or for hot springs)
- Waterproof bag/phone case (for bamboo rafting)
- Quick-dry towel (for rafting or hiking)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a visa to visit Yangshuo?
Most nationalities need a visa to visit China. However, citizens of 55+ countries can use the **144-hour visa-free transit policy** when transiting through Guilin (the nearest international airport) to a third country. Check our [complete China visa guide](/posts/ultimate-guide-to-china-visa-for-tourists/) for details.

### How many days do I need in Yangshuo?
2 days is the minimum to see the highlights (Yulong River rafting, cycling, Moon Hill, West Street). 3-4 days allows for a more relaxed pace, day trips to Xingping or Fuli, a cooking class, and more time to explore the countryside. Many travelers end up staying longer than planned—Yangshuo has a way of growing on you.

### Should I stay in Yangshuo or Guilin?
**Stay in Yangshuo** if: You want the best scenery, love cycling/hiking/rafting, prefer a laid-back countryside atmosphere, and plan to spend most of your time outdoors. Yangshuo is the better base for most travelers.

**Stay in Guilin** if: You want easy access to city attractions (Reed Flute Cave, Elephant Trunk Hill), good transport links, and more restaurant/shopping options. Most people only spend 1 night in Guilin before moving to Yangshuo.

**Best of both:** Spend 1 night in Guilin, then 2-3 nights in Yangshuo.

### Is the Yulong River bamboo raft worth it?
Yes, absolutely. The Yulong River bamboo raft is one of the best experiences in Yangshuo—much more peaceful and intimate than the crowded Li River cruise. Floating down the river past karst peaks, rice fields, and ancient villages is unforgettable. The classic combo (cycle to the start, raft down, cycle back) is the perfect Yangshuo day. Go early to avoid crowds.

### Is Yangshuo safe for solo travelers?
Yes, Yangshuo is very safe for solo travelers, including women. The area is tourist-friendly and violent crime is rare. The large expat and traveler community makes it easy to meet people. Use common sense with pickpockets in crowded areas, be careful when cycling/hiking, and use licensed operators. The locals are generally friendly and helpful.

### What should I eat in Yangshuo?
Must-try dishes: Beer fish (啤酒鱼)—Yangshuo's signature dish, Guilin rice noodles (桂林米粉), oil tea (油茶), stuffed Li River snails, bamboo rice, and osmanthus cake. Thanks to the expat community, Yangshuo also has excellent Western food—pizza, burgers, craft beer, and good coffee are all readily available.

### Can I visit Yangshuo as a day trip from Guilin?
Yes, you can visit Yangshuo as a day trip from Guilin (1.5 hours by bus), but it's not recommended. Yangshuo is best experienced over 2-3 days—there's too much to see and do in a single day, and the laid-back atmosphere is part of the appeal. If you're short on time, at least stay one night to experience West Street at night and the countryside in the morning.

### Is Yangshuo too touristy?
Yangshuo can be touristy, especially West Street and the main attractions. But it's easy to escape the crowds—cycle into the countryside, take a bamboo raft early in the morning, or visit nearby villages like Xingping and Fuli. The karst scenery is stunning regardless of crowds, and the laid-back atmosphere still shines through. Go in the off-season (winter) for fewer crowds.

### What's the best way to get around Yangshuo?
**Bicycle** is the best way to get around Yangshuo. The terrain is flat, the roads are scenic, and renting a bike is cheap (¥10-20/day). For longer distances or if you don't want to pedal, an **electric bike** (¥30-50/day) is a great option. Buses and taxis are available in town, but less convenient for exploring the countryside.

### Is Yangshuo worth visiting?
Absolutely. Yangshuo offers some of the most spectacular natural scenery in China, combined with a laid-back traveler atmosphere, great outdoor activities, and excellent food. Whether you're cycling through rice fields at sunset, floating down the Yulong River on a bamboo raft, or sipping coffee in a West Street cafe, Yangshuo is a place that stays with you. It's one of China's most beloved travel destinations for good reason.
