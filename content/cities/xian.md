Xi'an is China's original capital. Thirteen dynasties ruled from here over 1,100 years, and you feel that weight everywhere—from the massive Ming Dynasty city wall to the army of terracotta soldiers standing guard outside the city. But Xi'an isn't just a museum piece. The Muslim Quarter buzzes with street food energy, and the city's mix of Hui, Han, and Silk Road influences makes it feel distinct from anywhere else in China.

As the eastern terminus of the ancient Silk Road, Xi'an has been a crossroads of cultures for over 2,000 years. This is where Chinese civilization meets Central Asia, where Buddhist pagodas stand beside Muslim mosques, and where noodles and lamb skewers rule the food scene.

## Top-rated Time to Visit Xi'an

Xi'an has a temperate continental climate with four distinct seasons:

- **March to May (Spring):** Excellent time. Temperatures 10-25°C (50-77°F), flowers blooming, comfortable for sightseeing.
- **September to November (Autumn):** Top-rated time. Clear skies, comfortable temperatures (10-22°C/50-72°F), and the city's ginkgo trees turn golden in late October.
- **June to August (Summer):** Hot (30-38°C/86-100°F) and dry. The Terracotta Warriors pits are not air-conditioned—go early.
- **December to February (Winter):** Cold (-5 to 5°C/23-41°F) and dry. Few tourists, hotel prices low. The city wall is beautiful in snow.

**Avoid:** Chinese New Year (late January/early February), the first week of October (National Day holiday), and mid-July to mid-August (extreme heat).

## Getting There & Around

### Airport

- **XIY (Xi'an Xianyang International Airport):** 47km northwest of city center. The largest airport in northwest China.
  - **Metro Line 14:** Direct to city center (Beikezhan station) in ~50 minutes. ¥7-10.
  - **Airport Bus:** Multiple routes to major hotels and train stations. ¥25-30.
  - **Taxi/Didi:** ¥120-180 to city center, 45-60 minutes.

### High-Speed Rail

Xi'an is a major high-speed rail hub:
- **Xi'an North Station (西安北):** Main high-speed rail station, connected to Metro Line 2/4/14.
  - To Beijing: 4.5-6 hours, ¥515-1700
  - To Shanghai: 6-8 hours, ¥670-2200
  - To Chengdu: 3-4 hours, ¥263-800
  - To Luoyang: 30-40 minutes, ¥60-200
- **Xi'an Station (西安):** Older station, closer to city center. Regular trains and some high-speed trains.

### Metro

Xi'an has 8 metro lines covering most major sights. Fares start at ¥2.

- **Payment:** Alipay/WeChat transit QR code or Xi'an Metro Card.
- **Hours:** 6:00 AM to 11:00 PM (varies by line).
- **Key lines:** Line 2 (north-south, Bell Tower, City Wall), Line 4 (Big Wild Goose Pagoda, Muslim Quarter area), Line 9 (Terracotta Warriors).

### Bike the City Wall

The ancient city wall is 14km around and completely intact. Rent a bike on top and cycle the whole thing in 1.5-2 hours. **Top-rated at sunset** when the city lights come on and the temperature is cooler.

- **Tickets:** ¥54 (wall entry) + ¥45 (single bike rental for 3 hours) / ¥90 (tandem bike).
- **Top-rated entrance:** South Gate (Yongningmen) for a top choice views and facilities.
- **Hours:** 8:00 AM to 10:00 PM (summer), 8:00 AM to 8:00 PM (winter).
- **Pro tip:** Bring water—there are few shops on the wall. Wear comfortable shoes.

## Must-See Sights

### Terracotta Warriors (兵马俑)

![Terracotta Warriors rows](/img/china-dest/xian/terracotta-warriors-rows.webp)

One of the world's greatest archaeological finds. Discovered in 1974 by farmers digging a well, this is the mausoleum complex of Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor of unified China. Three pits contain over 8,000 life-sized clay soldiers, 130 chariots, and 670 horses—each with unique facial features.

- **Tickets:** ¥120 (includes all three pits and the museum). Book in advance via official WeChat mini-program.
- **When to go:** **Early morning (8:30 AM opening)**. The pits are not air-conditioned and get crowded by 11 AM. Allow 3-4 hours.
- **Getting there:**
  - **Metro Line 9** to Terracotta Army Station (兵马俑站), then shuttle bus (¥5) or walk 10 minutes. ~1.5 hours from city center.
  - **Bus 306 (游5)** from Xi'an Railway Station East Square. ¥7, 1-1.5 hours. Look for the official green buses—avoid the fake "tourist buses" that try to scam you.
  - **Didi/taxi:** ¥150-200 one way, 45-60 minutes.
- **What to see:**
  - **Pit 1:** The largest and most impressive. 6,000+ soldiers in battle formation. The first pit discovered and the most restored.
  - **Pit 2:** Contains cavalry, infantry, and archers in tactical formation. Less restored but shows the excavation process.
  - **Pit 3:** The command center. Smallest pit, contains high-ranking officers and a war chariot.
  - **Museum:** Exhibits on the history, excavation, and restoration process. Don't miss the two bronze chariots (replicas of the originals found in the emperor's tomb).
- **Pro tips:**
  - **Hire a guide** (¥100-200 for 2-3 hours) at the entrance or use an audio guide (¥40). The context makes it 10x better.
  - Pit 1 is best viewed from the back (fewer crowds, better perspective).
  - No flash photography.
  - Bring water and snacks—limited food options inside.
  - The actual emperor's tomb (Mount Li) is nearby but unexcavated. You can see the mound from a distance.

### Ancient City Wall (西安城墙)

The most intact ancient city wall in China and one of the largest military fortifications in the world. 14km of Ming Dynasty (1370 AD) brick and stone, 12m high, 15-18m thick at the base.

- **Tickets:** ¥54.
- **When to go:** Late afternoon (4-6 PM) for sunset views, or evening when the wall is lit up.
- **Allow:** 2-3 hours for a bike ride, 4-5 hours to walk.
- **Top-rated entrance:** South Gate (Yongningmen) for a top choice facilities, bike rental, and views.
- **Activities:**
  - **Biking:** Single bike ¥45/3hrs, tandem ¥90/3hrs. The most popular way to experience the wall.
  - **Walking:** Free with entry ticket. The full 14km takes 4-5 hours.
  - **Electric cart:** ¥80 per person for a full loop with stops.
- **Pro tip:** Combine with a visit to the Bell Tower and Drum Tower nearby. The South Gate area has great views of the modern city skyline.

### Muslim Quarter (回民街)

The food capital of Xi'an and the heart of the city's Hui Muslim community. A maze of narrow streets and alleys filled with food stalls, restaurants, mosques, and souvenir shops. Hui Muslim culture meets Central Asian spice routes here.

- **Tickets:** Free.
- **When to go:** Evening (6-10 PM) for a top choice atmosphere and food selection. Weekday evenings are less crowded than weekends.
- **Allow:** 2-3 hours for eating and exploring.
- **Must-try foods:**
  - **Yangroupaomo (羊肉泡馍):** Lamb and bread soup. You break the flatbread into small pieces yourself, then the kitchen adds lamb broth. Xi'an's most famous dish. Try at Lao Sun Jia (老孙家) or Tong Sheng Xiang (同盛祥).
  - **Roujiamo (肉夹馍):** Chinese "hamburger"—braised pork or lamb chopped and stuffed in a baked flatbread. ¥10-15. Try at Jiasan (贾三) or any local shop.
  - **Liangpi (凉皮):** Cold skin noodles with chili oil, vinegar, and garlic. Excellent for summer. ¥8-12.
  - **Biangbiang noodles (裤带面):** Wide, hand-pulled noodles (as wide as a belt) with chili oil and vinegar. The character "biang" is the most complex in Chinese.
  - **Fresh pomegranate juice (石榴汁):** Xi'an is famous for pomegranates. Fresh-squeezed, ¥10-15 a cup.
  - **Lamb skewers (羊肉串):** Grilled lamb with cumin and chili. ¥3-5 per skewer.
  - **Persimmon cakes (黄桂柿子饼):** Fried cakes made with persimmon and glutinous rice. Sweet and sticky.
- **Pro tips:**
  - Go hungry—you'll want to try everything.
  - The main street (Beiyuanmen) is the most touristy. Venture into the side alleys for better, cheaper food.
  - Bring cash—many stalls don't accept mobile payment (though most do now).
  - The Great Mosque (大清真寺) is nearby and worth a visit (¥25).

### Shaanxi History Museum (陕西历史博物馆)

One of a top choice museums in China, with over 370,000 artifacts spanning 1,000,000 years of history. The collection includes bronze ware, pottery, gold and silver, murals, and artifacts from the Zhou, Qin, Han, and Tang dynasties.

- **Tickets:** Free (basic exhibition), but **requires advance reservation** via official WeChat mini-program (released 7 days in advance, sells out fast). Special exhibitions cost ¥30-300.
- **When to go:** Morning (9 AM opening). Allow 2-3 hours.
- **Don't miss:**
  - **Bronze gallery:** Shang and Zhou dynasty bronze vessels with intricate patterns.
  - **Terracotta warriors:** A few examples from the pits (better to see the real thing at the actual site).
  - **Tang dynasty gold and silver:** Incredible craftsmanship from the height of the Silk Road.
  - **Mural gallery (special exhibition):** Tang dynasty tomb murals, some of a top choice preserved in China.
- **Pro tip:** Book tickets at least 3-5 days in advance. If you can't get free tickets, the special exhibition tickets (¥30) include access to the basic galleries.

### Big Wild Goose Pagoda (大雁塔)

A Tang Dynasty (652 AD) Buddhist pagoda built to house the Buddhist scriptures brought back from India by the monk Xuanzang (the real-life inspiration for Journey to the West). 64m tall, 7 stories.

- **Tickets:** ¥50 (pagoda + temple) or ¥30 (temple only, pagoda view from outside).
- **When to go:** Late afternoon, then stay for the fountain show.
- **Allow:** 1-2 hours.
- **North Square Fountain Show:** The largest musical fountain in Asia. Free. Shows at 12:00 PM, 4:00 PM, 8:00 PM (weekends add 10:00 AM, 2:00 PM, 6:00 PM). The 8 PM show is a top choice with lights. Arrive 30 minutes early for a good spot.
- **Pro tip:** The pagoda area is surrounded by the Tang Paradise (大唐不夜城) pedestrian street, which is beautiful at night with Tang Dynasty-themed architecture, statues, and performances.

### Bell Tower & Drum Tower (钟楼鼓楼)

The symbolic center of Xi'an. The Bell Tower (1384 AD) is at the intersection of the four main streets of the old city. The Drum Tower (1380 AD) is nearby, overlooking the Muslim Quarter.

- **Tickets:** ¥30 each, or ¥50 for both.
- **When to go:** Evening when they're lit up. You can see them from the street for free—going inside is optional.
- **Allow:** 30-60 minutes for both.
- **Pro tip:** The Bell Tower is best viewed from the underground pedestrian tunnel that surrounds it. The Drum Tower has great views over the Muslim Quarter.

## Where to Eat

Xi'an's food scene is dominated by noodles, lamb, and Central Asian influences. Here's where to eat:

### Classic Xi'an Dishes
- **Yangroupaomo (羊肉泡馍):** Lamb and bread soup. The iconic Xi'an dish. Try at Lao Sun Jia (老孙家) or Tong Sheng Xiang (同盛祥). ¥40-60.
- **Roujiamo (肉夹馍):** Braised meat in flatbread. The original hamburger. Try at Jiasan (贾三) or any local shop. ¥10-15.
- **Liangpi (凉皮):** Cold noodles with chili oil. Refreshing and spicy. ¥8-12.
- **Biangbiang noodles (裤带面):** Wide hand-pulled noodles. ¥15-25.
- **Spicy and sour soup (胡辣汤):** Breakfast soup with meatballs, vegetables, and pepper. ¥8-12.
- **Osmanthus cake (桂花糕):** Sweet rice cake with osmanthus honey. Street food dessert. ¥5-10.

### Restaurants
- **Lao Sun Jia (老孙家):** Famous for yangroupaomo. Multiple locations. Touristy but authentic. ¥50-80 per person.
- **Jiasan (贾三):** Top-rated roujiamo and steamed dumplings in the Muslim Quarter. ¥30-50 per person.
- **Shengxingzhai (盛志望):** Local Xi'an cuisine, less touristy. Famous for maocai (冒菜) and noodles. ¥30-50 per person.
- **Wei Jia Sauce Bone (魏家酱骨头):** Local chain for braised pork bones and noodles. Good value. ¥30-50 per person.
- **Rosewood Restaurant (玫瑰餐厅):** Upscale Shaanxi cuisine in a beautiful setting. ¥150-300 per person.

### Street Food
- **Muslim Quarter:** The best street food area. Try everything—lamb skewers, pomegranate juice, persimmon cakes, roujiamo, liangpi.
- **Sajin Bridge (洒金桥):** Less touristy than the main Muslim Quarter street. Locals eat here. Try the beef cakes, fried rice cakes, and lamb soup.
- **East Street (东大街):** More modern food street with restaurants and snack shops.

## Where to Stay

### Near Bell Tower / Muslim Quarter (莲湖区)
- **Top-rated for:** First-time visitors, food, central location.
- **Price range:** ¥300-1000/night.
- **Pros:** Walk to Muslim Quarter, Bell Tower, Drum Tower, and City Wall. Top-rated food area.
- **Cons:** Can be noisy and crowded. Some areas are run down.

### Near Big Wild Goose Pagoda (雁塔区)
- **Top-rated for:** Quieter, more modern, business travelers.
- **Price range:** ¥400-1500/night.
- **Pros:** Modern area, close to Big Wild Goose Pagoda and Shaanxi History Museum, better hotels.
- **Cons:** Farther from the old city and Muslim Quarter (20-30 min by metro).

### Near City Wall South Gate (碑林区)
- **Top-rated for:** Culture, history, mid-range.
- **Price range:** ¥350-1200/night.
- **Pros:** Close to City Wall, Forest of Steles Museum, and South Gate. Good metro access.
- **Cons:** Some areas are commercial.

### Budget Options
- **Xi'an Han Tang House Hostel:** Near the Bell Tower, clean and social. ¥80-150/night for a dorm bed.
- **The Rooftop Hostel:** Near the Muslim Quarter, great rooftop views. ¥100-200/night.

- **3:30 PM:** Tang Paradise (大唐不夜城) pedestrian street (if time)

## 4-Day Itinerary Extension

If you have 2 more days:

### Day 3: Huaqing Hot Springs & Mount Li
- **9:00 AM:** Huaqing Hot Springs (华清池)—Tang Dynasty imperial hot springs, site of the famous Yang Guifei romance. ¥120.
- **12:00 PM:** Lunch at Huaqing
- **2:00 PM:** Mount Li (骊山)—hike or take cable car to the top for views of the Wei River valley. The site of the 1936 Xi'an Incident.
- **5:00 PM:** Return to Xi'an
- **7:00 PM:** Dinner + evening show "Everlasting Sorrow" (长恨歌) at Huaqing (if you stay late—highly recommended, ¥200-900)

### Day 4: Famen Temple or Hukou Waterfall
**Option A: Famen Temple (法门寺)**
- 1.5 hours by car/bus west of Xi'an
- Buddhist temple housing the finger bone relic of Buddha
- Massive modern temple complex + ancient pagoda
- ¥100, allow 4-5 hours including travel

**Option B: Hukou Waterfall (壶口瀑布)**
- 3-4 hours by car east of Xi'an (on the Yellow River)
- The second largest waterfall in China, spectacular when the Yellow River is full (summer)
- ¥90, allow full day (8-10 hours including travel)

## Practical Tips

### Money & Payments
- Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted everywhere, including most street food stalls in the Muslim Quarter.
- Carry ¥200-500 cash for small vendors, temples, and emergencies.
- ATMs are everywhere. Most accept international cards.

### Internet & VPN
- Google, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, YouTube are blocked.
- **Set up a VPN before arrival.** ExpressVPN, Astrill, and NordVPN work well.
- Xi'an has good 4G/5G coverage.

### Language
- English is not widely spoken outside major hotels and tourist sites.
- Download Pleco (Chinese dictionary) and Google Translate (offline pack) for menus and directions.
- The Muslim Quarter has many Hui vendors who may speak some Arabic or Central Asian languages, but Mandarin is universal.

### Safety
- Xi'an is very safe. Violent crime is rare.
- Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas (Muslim Quarter, metro, train stations).
- Be careful of fake "tourist buses" at Xi'an Railway Station—only take the official green Bus 306 (游5).
- The Terracotta Warriors area has some scam artists offering "private tours"—book guides at the official counter inside the site.

### Health
- Xi'an is drier than eastern China—stay hydrated and use moisturizer.
- The food is spicy and oily—if you have a sensitive stomach, start mild and carry stomach medicine.
- Drink bottled or boiled water only.
- The Terracotta Warriors pits can be dusty—people with allergies may want a mask.

### Tipping
- Tipping is not customary. No need to tip at restaurants, food stalls, or for taxis.
- Guides at the Terracotta Warriors may appreciate a small tip, but it's not expected.

### What to Pack
- Comfortable walking shoes (you'll walk a lot, especially at the Terracotta Warriors and on the city wall)
- Light layers (dry climate, temperature varies between day and night)
- Portable power bank (essential for navigation and payments)
- Universal adapter (Type A, C, I, 220V)
- Sunscreen and hat (Xi'an is sunny and dry, especially in spring and summer)
- Moisturizer and lip balm (dry climate)
- Toilet paper (many public restrooms don't provide it)
- Hand sanitizer
- Stomach medicine (for the spicy food!)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a visa to visit Xi'an?
Most nationalities need a visa to visit China. However, citizens of 55+ countries can use the **144-hour visa-free transit policy** when transiting through Xi'an to a third country. Check our [complete China visa guide](/posts/ultimate-guide-to-china-visa-for-tourists/) for details.

### How many days do I need in Xi'an?
2 days is the minimum to see the highlights (Terracotta Warriors, City Wall, Muslim Quarter, museum). 3-4 days allows for day trips to Huaqing Hot Springs, Famen Temple, or Hukou Waterfall. Many travelers combine Xi'an with Beijing (4.5-6 hours by high-speed train) or Chengdu (3-4 hours).

### How do I get from Xi'an to the Terracotta Warriors?
- **Metro Line 9** to Terracotta Army Station (兵马俑站), then shuttle bus (¥5) or walk 10 minutes. ~1.5 hours from city center, ¥7-10.
- **Bus 306 (游5)** from Xi'an Railway Station East Square. ¥7, 1-1.5 hours. Look for the official green buses.
- **Didi/taxi:** ¥150-200 one way, 45-60 minutes. Easiest option for early morning visits.

### Do I need a guide at the Terracotta Warriors?
While you can visit without a guide, **we highly recommend hiring one** (¥100-200 for 2-3 hours) or using an audio guide (¥40). The terracotta warriors are impressive to look at, but the history, archaeology, and significance make them 10x more interesting with context. Book guides at the official counter inside the entrance, not from scalpers outside.

### Is the Muslim Quarter safe for tourists?
Yes, the Muslim Quarter is very safe. It's a popular tourist area with lots of people and police presence. Use common sense with pickpockets in crowds, and be careful of your belongings. The food is generally safe—stick to busy stalls with high turnover.

### What's a top choice time to visit the Terracotta Warriors?
**Early morning (8:30 AM opening)** is a top choice time. The pits are not air-conditioned and get hot and crowded by 11 AM. Going early means cooler temperatures, fewer crowds, and better photos. **Autumn (September-November)** and **spring (March-May)** are a top choice seasons for comfortable weather.

### Can I visit the Terracotta Warriors and Huaqing Hot Springs in one day?
Yes, they're only 15km apart. Most people visit both in one day:
- Morning: Terracotta Warriors (3-4 hours)
- Afternoon: Huaqing Hot Springs (2-3 hours)
- Evening: "Everlasting Sorrow" show at Huaqing (if time)
Take Bus 306 (游5) which stops at both sites, or hire a car/driver for the day (¥400-600).

### Is Xi'an food very spicy?
Xi'an food is less spicy than Sichuan food, but it can still be spicy. The signature flavors are **cumin, chili, and vinegar** (from Central Asian/Silk Road influences) rather than the numbing Sichuan peppercorn. Most dishes can be ordered mild (不辣) or medium (微辣). Start mild and work your way up.

### What should I eat in Xi'an?
Must-try dishes: yangroupaomo (lamb bread soup), roujiamo (meat sandwich), liangpi (cold noodles), biangbiang noodles, lamb skewers, fresh pomegranate juice, persimmon cakes, and spicy and sour soup for breakfast. The Muslim Quarter is a top choice place to try everything.

### Is Xi'an worth visiting?
Absolutely. Xi'an is one of China's most historically significant cities and offers a completely different experience from Beijing or Shanghai. The Terracotta Warriors are one of the world's greatest archaeological wonders, the city wall is unique, and the Muslim Quarter food scene is among the top-rated in China. If you're interested in Chinese history, archaeology, or food, Xi'an is a must-visit.
