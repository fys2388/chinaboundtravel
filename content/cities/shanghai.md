Shanghai doesn't do subtle. It's a city of superlatives—second-tallest building in the world, busiest container port, longest metro network. But beyond the skyline spectacle, you'll find quiet lanes of French plane trees, dumpling shops older than your grandparents, and a nightlife scene that rivals Tokyo.

This is China's most international city, where East meets West in the most dramatic way possible. From the colonial-era Bund to the sci-fi skyline of Pudong, Shanghai is a city that never stops moving—and never stops surprising.

![Shanghai skyline at golden hour](/img/china-dest/shanghai/shanghai-skyline-golden-hour.webp)

## Top-rated Time to Visit Shanghai

Shanghai has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons:

- **March to May (Spring):** The best time. Temperatures 15-25°C (59-77°F), cherry blossoms bloom in late March, and the city is lush and green.
- **September to November (Autumn):** Second best. Clear skies, comfortable temperatures, and the city's plane trees turn golden.
- **June to August (Summer):** Hot and brutally humid (30-38°C/86-100°F). June is the "plum rain" season with constant drizzle. Avoid if possible.
- **December to February (Winter):** Cold (0-10°C/32-50°F) and damp. No central heating in most buildings. But hotel prices are lowest and crowds are minimal.

**Avoid:** Chinese New Year (late January/early February), the first week of October (National Day holiday), and mid-June to mid-July (plum rain season).

## Getting There & Around

### Airports

- **PVG (Pudong International Airport):** Main international hub, 30km east of city center.
  - **Maglev Train:** Hits 431 km/h (268 mph) and gets you to Longyang Road metro station in 8 minutes. ¥50 one-way (¥40 if you have a same-day flight ticket). The coolest way to arrive, but only connects to Line 2/7/16.
  - **Metro Line 2:** Budget option, ~1 hour to People's Square. ¥7-9. Runs 6:00-22:00.
  - **Taxi/Didi:** ¥150-250 to city center, 45-90 minutes depending on traffic.
- **SHA (Hongqiao International Airport):** Mostly domestic flights, but some regional international (Japan, Korea, Southeast Asia). 13km west of city center.
  - **Metro Line 2/10:** Direct to city center, 30-45 minutes. ¥5-7.
  - **Taxi/Didi:** ¥50-100, 20-40 minutes.
  - **Hongqiao Railway Station** is adjacent—excellent if you're taking the high-speed train to Beijing, Hangzhou, or Suzhou.

### Metro

Shanghai's metro is the longest in the world (over 800km/500mi), clean, efficient, and covers basically everything. Fares start at ¥3.

- **Payment:** Shanghai Public Transport Card (¥20 deposit) or Alipay/WeChat transit QR code.
- **Hours:** 5:30 AM to 10:30 PM (varies by line).
- **Tip:** Avoid rush hour (7:30-9:30 AM, 5:00-7:00 PM) if possible—Lines 1, 2, and 8 get extremely crowded.

### Ferries

The ¥2 ferry across the Huangpu River (Dongchang Road to Jinling Road East) is one of a top choice-value experiences in China. Take it at sunset for incredible views of both the Bund and Pudong skyline. There are also ¥100-150 cruise boats if you want a longer ride, but the local ferry is more authentic.

### Didi & Taxis

Didi is cheap and reliable. A typical cross-city ride costs ¥30-80. Taxis are also plentiful—flag them down or book via Didi. Make sure the driver uses the meter (计价器).

## Must-See Sights

### The Bund (Waitan)

![The Bund at night](/img/china-dest/shanghai/shanghai-bund-night-walk.jpg)

The colonial-era waterfront promenade facing Pudong's sci-fi skyline. This is Shanghai's iconic postcard view—52 buildings of various architectural styles (Gothic, Baroque, Neoclassical, Art Deco) on one side, and the world's most futuristic skyline on the other.

- **When to go:** Go at night (7-10 PM) when everything lights up. The light show on the Pudong buildings runs every evening. Sunrise is also beautiful and much less crowded.
- **How to get there:** Metro Line 2/10 to East Nanjing Road, then walk 10 minutes east.
- **Allow:** 1-2 hours for a stroll.
- **Pro tip:** Walk north from the Peace Hotel to Waibaidu Bridge for a top choice views. The area between the Bund and Suzhou Creek has some great rooftop bars.

### Pudong Skyline

The futuristic financial district across the river. Three supertall skyscrapers dominate:

1. **Shanghai Tower (632m):** World's second-tallest building. Observation deck on floor 118 (¥180). The fastest elevator in the world (18m/s) gets you up in 55 seconds. On clear days you can see forever.
2. **Shanghai World Financial Center (492m):** The "bottle opener." Observation deck on floor 100 (¥160) has a glass floor section.
3. **Jin Mao Tower (421m):** The pagoda-shaped one. Observation deck on floor 88 (¥120).

**Tip:** Pick one—they all offer similar views. Shanghai Tower is the tallest and newest. Go on a clear day (check visibility before buying tickets).

### Yu Garden (Yuyuan)

Classic Chinese garden in the Old City, built in 1559 during the Ming Dynasty. Beautiful pavilions, rockeries, ponds, and zigzag bridges. Surrounded by the Yu Garden Bazaar—a tourist market with traditional architecture, shops, and restaurants.

- **Tickets:** ¥40 (peak season) / ¥30 (off-season).
- **When to go:** Early morning (9 AM opening) to beat the crowds. The garden is small and gets packed by 11 AM.
- **Allow:** 1-2 hours for the garden, plus time for the bazaar.
- **Pro tip:** Don't buy souvenirs in the bazaar—they're overpriced. But do try the Nanxiang Mantou Dian for xiaolongbao (expect a long queue).

### Former French Concession

![French Concession street](/img/china-dest/shanghai/french-concession-street.webp)

Tree-lined streets, Art Deco architecture, and some of Shanghai's best cafes, boutiques, and bars. This is the most charming part of the city—and a top choice area to just wander.

**Highlights:**
- **Wukang Road:** The most famous street in the French Concession. Wukang Mansion (Normandie Apartments) at the corner is an iconic Art Deco building. Lined with cafes, boutiques, and celebrity homes.
- **Anfu Road:** Trendy street with independent boutiques, cafes, and the Shanghai Dramatic Arts Centre.
- **Xingguo Road:** Quiet, residential, less touristy. Beautiful plane trees and old villas.
- **Hengshan Road:** Once the main bar street, now more upscale with restaurants and hotels.
- **Fuxing Park:** The largest park in the French Concession. Great for people-watching—locals do tai chi, dance, and play cards.

**How to explore:** Walk or rent a bicycle. The area is flat and the tree canopy provides shade. Get lost in the small lanes—that's where you'll find a top choice cafes and hidden courtyards.

### Tianzifang

Arts and crafts alleyways in the former French Concession (near Dapuqiao metro). A maze of narrow lanes converted into art galleries, boutique shops, cafes, and restaurants. Touristy but charming.

- **Tickets:** Free entry.
- **When to go:** Afternoon or evening. Weekday mornings are quietest.
- **Allow:** 1-2 hours.
- **Pro tip:** Great for photos and people-watching. The side lanes have better, less touristy cafes.

### Nanjing Road

![Nanjing Road street](/img/china-dest/shanghai/nanjing-road-street.webp)

The world's longest shopping street (5.5km). East Nanjing Road (near the Bund) is the neon-drenched, touristy pedestrian mall with flagship stores and street performers. West Nanjing Road is more upscale with malls like Plaza 66 and Jing An Kerry Centre.

- **When to go:** Evening, when the neon lights are on. It's chaotic and crowded, worth one walkthrough.
- **Pro tip:** Don't buy anything on East Nanjing Road—it's all tourist traps. Go there for the spectacle, then shop elsewhere.

### Jing'an Temple

A stunning Buddhist temple in the middle of the city's most modern district. The golden roof contrasts dramatically with the surrounding skyscrapers. Originally built in 247 AD, the current building is a modern reconstruction.

- **Tickets:** ¥50 (includes incense).
- **When to go:** Morning. The temple is active with worshippers.
- **Allow:** 30-60 minutes.

## Where to Eat

Shanghai's food scene is incredible—from street breakfast stalls to 3-Michelin-starred restaurants. Here's what to eat and where:

### Soup Dumplings (Xiaolongbao)

Shanghai's signature dish. Thin-skinned dumplings filled with pork and hot soup. Eat carefully—bite a small hole first, sip the soup, then eat the dumpling. Dip in vinegar and ginger.

- **Jia Jia Tang Bao (佳家汤包):** Locals' favorite. Tiny shop near People's Square. Expect to queue. ¥30-50 per person.
- **Fu Chun (富春):** Old-school dim sum restaurant in Jing'an. Great xiaolongbao and other Shanghai dim sum. ¥50-80 per person.
- **Din Tai Fung (鼎泰丰):** Taiwanese chain, reliable and consistent. Multiple locations. More expensive (¥100-150 per person) but English menu and clean.
- **Nanxiang Mantou Dian (南翔馒头店):** The original xiaolongbao restaurant in Yu Garden. Touristy but authentic. ¥80-120 per person.

### Shanghai Breakfast

- **Cifantuan (粢饭团):** Sticky rice roll with youtiao (fried dough), sugar, and sometimes pork floss. ¥5-10 from street vendors.
- **Da bing (大饼):** Scallion pancake, crispy and savory. ¥3-5.
- **Youtiao (油条):** Fried dough stick, eaten with congee or dipped in soy milk. ¥2-3.
- **Shengjianbao (生煎包):** Pan-fried pork buns with crispy bottoms. ¥15-25 for 4. Try at Yang's Dumplings (小杨生煎), a local chain.
- **Xiaolongbao:** Yes, people eat them for breakfast too.

### Local Specialties

- **Red-Braised Pork (红烧肉):** Slow-cooked pork belly in soy sauce and sugar. Sweet, savory, melt-in-your-mouth.
- **Sweet and Sour Spare Ribs (糖醋排骨):** Tangy and sweet, a Shanghai classic.
- **Eight-Treasure Duck (八宝鸭):** Whole duck stuffed with glutinous rice, mushrooms, chestnuts, and other ingredients. A festive dish.
- **Scallion Oil Noodles (葱油拌面):** Simple but excellent—noodles with scallion oil and soy sauce. ¥15-25 at any local noodle shop.

### Fine Dining

- **Ultraviolet by Paul Pairet:** 3 Michelin stars. Immersive 10-course dining experience with custom lighting, music, and visuals for each dish. ¥6,000+ per person. Book months ahead.
- **Jia Jia Tang Bao:** Not fine dining, but a top choice xiaolongbao in the city.
- **Lost Heaven (花马天堂):** Yunnanese cuisine in a beautiful space. Great for a special dinner. ¥200-400 per person.

### Late Night

- **Changle Road:** Bar-hopping street in the French Concession. Craft cocktails, speakeasies, and dive bars.
- **Yongkang Road:** Once the main bar street, now quieter but still has a few good spots.
- **Found 158:** Underground nightlife complex with clubs and bars. Popular with expats.

## Where to Stay

Shanghai is huge—where you stay matters. The best areas for tourists are:

### The Bund / Huangpu
- **Top-rated for:** First-time visitors, iconic views, luxury.
- **Price range:** ¥800-3000+/night.
- **Pros:** Walk to the Bund, Yu Garden, and Nanjing Road. Top-rated skyline views.
- **Cons:** Expensive, can be touristy.

### Former French Concession (Xuhui / Huangpu)
- **Top-rated for:** Charm, cafes, boutique shopping, food.
- **Price range:** ¥500-2000/night.
- **Pros:** Walkable, tree-lined streets, best restaurants and bars, most "European" feel.
- **Cons:** Far from the Bund (20-30 min by metro).

### Jing'an
- **Top-rated for:** Central location, shopping, well-connected.
- **Price range:** ¥400-1500/night.
- **Pros:** Metro hub (Lines 2, 7, 12, 13), Jing'an Temple, great malls and restaurants, mix of budgets.
- **Cons:** Busy and commercial.

### Pudong (Lujiazui)
- **Top-rated for:** Business travelers, skyline views, modern hotels.
- **Price range:** ¥600-2500/night.
- **Pros:** Modern hotels with great views, close to Shanghai Tower and other skyscrapers.
- **Cons:** Far from the historic center (30-45 min by metro), less charm, expensive food.

### Budget Options
- **The Hideout Hostel:** Clean, social hostel in the French Concession. ¥100-200/night for a dorm bed.
- **Shanghai Blue Mountain Youth Hostel:** Near the Bund, good location. ¥80-150/night.

## 3-Day Itinerary

### Day 1: Colonial Shanghai & The Bund
- **9:00 AM:** Yu Garden (arrive at opening to beat crowds)
- **11:00 AM:** Yu Garden Bazaar + Nanxiang Mantou Dian for xiaolongbao
- **1:00 PM:** Shanghai Museum (free, excellent ancient Chinese art collection) OR Shanghai History Museum
- **3:00 PM:** East Nanjing Road stroll (spectacle, not shopping)
- **5:00 PM:** The Bund (golden hour)
- **6:30 PM:** Huangpu River ferry at sunset (¥2!)
- **7:30 PM:** The Bund at night (light show)
- **8:30 PM:** Dinner near the Bund or in Pudong

### Day 2: French Concession & Modern Shanghai
- **9:00 AM:** Jing'an Temple
- **10:30 AM:** Walk through the Former French Concession (Wukang Road → Anfu Road → Fuxing Park)
- **12:30 PM:** Lunch in the French Concession (try Jia Jia Tang Bao or a local cafe)
- **2:30 PM:** Tianzifang (art lanes, cafes, shopping)
- **4:30 PM:** Shanghai Tower observation deck (go late afternoon for both day and night views)
- **7:00 PM:** Dinner in Pudong or back in the French Concession
- **9:00 PM:** Bar-hopping on Changle Road or Yongkang Road

### Day 3: Day Trip or Deep Dive

**Option A: Zhujiajiao Water Town**
- **9:00 AM:** Take Metro Line 17 to Zhujiajiao (1.5 hours)
- **10:30 AM - 3:00 PM:** Explore the ancient water town—canals, stone bridges, old houses, boat rides (¥80)
- **1:00 PM:** Lunch at a local restaurant (try the river shrimp and zongzi)
- **4:00 PM:** Return to Shanghai
- **6:00 PM:** Dinner and final shopping

**Option B: Stay Local**
- **Morning:** Shanghai Propaganda Poster Art Centre (hidden in a basement, fascinating collection) OR Longhua Temple
- **Afternoon:** Xintiandi (restored Shikumen houses, upscale shops and restaurants) OR China Art Museum (free, huge contemporary art museum)
- **Evening:** Acrobatics show at Shanghai Centre Theatre OR final dinner

## Practical Tips

### Money & Payments
- Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted everywhere. Shanghai is the most cashless city in China.
- Carry ¥300-500 cash for small vendors and emergencies.
- ATMs are everywhere. Most accept international cards.

### Internet & VPN
- Google, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, YouTube are blocked.
- **Set up a VPN before arrival.** ExpressVPN, Astrill, and NordVPN work well.
- Shanghai has free public WiFi in many areas (including the metro stations), but you'll still need a VPN for Western apps.

### Language
- Shanghai is the most foreigner-friendly city in China. English signage is common in tourist areas, hotels, and major restaurants.
- Still, download Pleco (Chinese dictionary) and Google Translate (offline pack) for menus and directions.
- Shanghainese (Wu dialect) is the local language, but everyone speaks Mandarin.

### Safety
- Shanghai is very safe. Violent crime is extremely rare.
- Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas (metro, Nanjing Road, the Bund).
- Be careful with taxi scams at the airport—use the official taxi queue or Didi.

### Tipping
- Tipping is not customary. No need to tip at restaurants, hotels, or for taxis.

### What to Pack
- Comfortable walking shoes (you'll walk a lot)
- Light layers (humid climate, temperature varies)
- Portable power bank (essential for navigation and payments)
- Universal adapter (Type A, C, I, 220V)
- Umbrella (sudden rain is common, especially in summer)
- Toilet paper (many public restrooms don't provide it)
- Hand sanitizer

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a visa to visit Shanghai?
Most nationalities need a visa. However, citizens of 55+ countries can use the **144-hour visa-free transit policy** when transiting through Shanghai to a third country. Shanghai also has a **24-hour visa-free transit** for most nationalities. Check our [China visa guide](/posts/ultimate-guide-to-china-visa-for-tourists/) for details.

### How many days do I need in Shanghai?
3 days is the minimum to see the highlights. 4-5 days allows for day trips (Suzhou, Zhujiajiao) and a more relaxed pace. Many travelers combine Shanghai with Beijing (high-speed train, 4.5 hours) or Hangzhou (1 hour).

### Is Shanghai expensive?
Shanghai is China's most expensive city, but still affordable by Western standards. Budget: ¥400-600/day (hostel, street food, metro). Mid-range: ¥1000-2000/day. Luxury: ¥3000+/day.

### What's a top choice area to stay in Shanghai?
For first-time visitors: **The Bund** (iconic, walk to major sights) or **Former French Concession** (charming, best food and cafes). For budget travelers: **Jing'an** (central, well-connected, more affordable).

### Can I use my credit card in Shanghai?
International credit cards are accepted at major hotels, upscale restaurants, and large malls. But most local shops, restaurants, and attractions only accept Alipay, WeChat Pay, or cash. Set up mobile payment before arrival.

### Is the Maglev train worth it?
The Maglev is cool (431 km/h!) but only worth it if you're going to/from Pudong Airport and want the experience. It only connects to Longyang Road station, not the city center. For most travelers, Metro Line 2 (¥7, 1 hour) is more practical.

### What should I eat in Shanghai?
Must-try dishes: xiaolongbao (soup dumplings), shengjianbao (pan-fried buns), red-braised pork, scallion oil noodles, hairy crab (seasonal, October-November), and youtiao with soy milk for breakfast.

### Is Shanghai safe for solo travelers?
Yes, Shanghai is one of the safest cities in the world for solo travelers, including women. The city is well-lit, public transport is safe, and violent crime is rare. Use common sense with pickpockets and scams.

### How do I get from Shanghai to Beijing?
High-speed train (G-train) from Shanghai Hongqiao to Beijing South: 4.5-6 hours, ¥550-1700 depending on class. Flights: 2-2.5 hours, ¥500-1500. The train is more comfortable and city-center to city-center.
