Hangzhou, the capital of Zhejiang Province, is known as the "Paradise on Earth" (上有天堂，下有苏杭) for its stunning natural scenery and rich cultural heritage. For over 1,000 years, poets, painters, and emperors have been captivated by West Lake's misty shores, willow-lined causeways, and pagoda-dotted hills.

But Hangzhou is more than just a pretty lake. It's one of China's most prosperous and livable cities—home to Alibaba's headquarters, a thriving tech scene, excellent food, and some of the best green tea in the world. Whether you're cycling around West Lake at dawn, sipping Longjing tea in a mountain village, or exploring ancient Buddhist temples hidden in the hills, Hangzhou offers a perfect blend of natural beauty, culture, and modern comfort.

## Best Time to Visit Hangzhou

Hangzhou has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons:

- **March to May (Spring):** **Best time.** Cherry blossoms, peach blossoms, and tulips bloom around West Lake. The Longjing tea harvest begins in late March. Temperatures 12-22°C (54-72°F). April can be rainy.
- **September to November (Autumn):** Second best. Cool weather (15-25°C/59-77°F), clear skies, and autumn foliage (red maple leaves around the lake in late November). Osmanthus flowers bloom in September, filling the city with their sweet fragrance.
- **June to August (Summer):** Hot (28-38°C/82-100°F) and humid. June is the "plum rain" season with constant drizzle. The lotus flowers bloom in West Lake in July-August, which is beautiful. Best for early morning activities before the heat.
- **December to February (Winter):** Cold (2-10°C/36-50°F) and damp. Few tourists, hotel prices low. The lake can be beautiful in snow (rare but magical).

**Avoid:** Chinese New Year (late January/early February), the first week of October (National Day holiday—West Lake gets extremely crowded), and mid-June to early July (plum rain season).

## Getting There & Around

### Airport

- **HGH (Hangzhou Xiaoshan International Airport):** 27km east of city center. One of China's busiest airports, with flights to most major Asian cities and some European/Australian destinations.
  - **Metro Line 1/7:** Direct to city center (Wulin Square) in ~50 minutes. ¥7-9.
  - **Airport Bus:** Multiple routes to downtown hotels and train stations. ¥20-30, 40-60 minutes.
  - **Taxi/Didi:** ¥100-150 to West Lake area, 30-45 minutes.

### High-Speed Rail

Hangzhou is a major high-speed rail hub:
- **Hangzhou East Station (杭州东):** Main high-speed rail station, connected to Metro Line 1/4/6/19.
  - To Shanghai: 45-60 minutes, ¥73-250
  - To Suzhou: 1-1.5 hours, ¥110-350
  - To Nanjing: 1-1.5 hours, ¥120-380
  - To Beijing: 4.5-6 hours, ¥600-1900
  - To Huangshan (Yellow Mountain): 1.5-2 hours, ¥100-300
- **Hangzhou Station (杭州城站):** Older station, closer to West Lake. Some high-speed and regular trains.

### Within the City

- **Metro:** Modern subway system with 12+ lines covering most major attractions. Fares start at ¥2. Alipay/WeChat transit QR code works.
- **Buses:** Extensive bus network. ¥1-2 per ride. The "Y" series tourist buses connect major attractions.
- **Bicycles:** Hangzhou is one of China's most bike-friendly cities. The public bike share system (Hangzhou Public Bicycle) is extensive and cheap (¥1/hour, first hour free with card). Shared bikes (Meituan, Hello) are everywhere. Perfect for West Lake.
- **Taxis/Didi:** Affordable. A typical cross-city ride costs ¥20-50. Didi is widely used.
- **West Lake Boats:** Electric boats, rowboats, and larger cruise boats on West Lake. ¥50-150 depending on type and duration.

## Must-See Sights

### West Lake (西湖)

The star attraction and the heart and soul of Hangzhou. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2011, West Lake has inspired Chinese poets and painters for over 1,000 years. The lake is 3.3km long, 2.8km wide, and surrounded by hills, pagodas, temples, gardens, and willow-lined causeways.

- **Tickets:** Free to walk around the lake. Some attractions (pagodas, temples) charge separate fees.
- **When to go:** **Early morning (6-8 AM)** for misty views, fewer crowds, and locals doing tai chi. Late afternoon for golden light. Evening for the illuminated pagodas and lakeside walks.
- **How to experience:**
  - **Cycle around the lake:** The full loop is ~15km, takes 2-3 hours. The most popular way to see the lake. Rent a bike (¥10-20/day) or use the public bike share.
  - **Walk the causeways:** Su Causeway (苏堤, 2.8km) and Bai Causeway (白堤, 1km) are pedestrian-only and beautiful.
  - **Boat cruise:** Electric boat to the Three Pools Mirroring the Moon (三潭印月) island. ¥55 including boat and island entry.
  - **Take the tourist train:** A small electric train that loops around the lake. ¥10 per segment.
- **The "Ten Scenes of West Lake" (西湖十景):**
  1. **Su Causeway Spring Dawn (苏堤春晓):** Best in spring, peach blossoms and willows.
  2. **Curved Yard and Lotus Pool (曲院风荷):** Best in summer, lotus flowers.
  3. **Autumn Moon over Calm Lake (平湖秋月):** Best on autumn nights, full moon reflections.
  4. **Remnant Snow on Broken Bridge (断桥残雪):** Best after snow (rare), the bridge appears broken.
  5. **Orioles Singing in Willows (柳浪闻莺):** Best in spring, willow trees and birds.
  6. **Viewing Fish at Flower Harbor (花港观鱼):** Koi ponds and peony garden.
  7. **Sunset Glow at Leifeng Pagoda (雷峰夕照):** Best at sunset, Leifeng Pagoda silhouetted.
  8. **Twin Peaks Piercing Clouds (双峰插云):** Two mountain peaks visible from the lake.
  9. **Evening Bell at Nanping Hill (南屏晚钟):** Jingci Temple bell at dusk.
  10. **Three Pools Mirroring the Moon (三潭印月):** Three stone pagodas in the lake, best seen by boat.
- **Pro tips:**
  - Rent a bike and cycle the lake at sunrise—unforgettable.
  - The east and north shores are more developed; the west and south shores are quieter and more natural.
  - Bring water and snacks—there are shops but they're overpriced.
  - Wear comfortable shoes—you'll walk a lot.
  - The lake is beautiful in all weather—rainy days create a misty, ink-painting atmosphere.

### Lingyin Temple (灵隐寺)

One of China's most famous and important Buddhist temples, founded in 328 AD during the Eastern Jin Dynasty. Nestled in the hills northwest of West Lake, the temple complex includes ancient halls, towering trees, and the famous Feilai Peak (飞来峰) with hundreds of Buddhist rock carvings.

- **Tickets:** ¥45 (Feilai Peak scenic area) + ¥30 (Lingyin Temple itself, must buy both to enter the temple). Total ¥75.
- **When to go:** Early morning (7-9 AM) for fewer crowds and a more spiritual atmosphere. The temple opens at 7 AM.
- **Allow:** 2-3 hours.
- **What to see:**
  - **Feilai Peak (飞来峰):** A limestone hill with over 470 Buddhist rock carvings dating from the 10th to 14th centuries. The most famous is the laughing Maitreya Buddha.
  - **Hall of the Heavenly Kings (天王殿):** The first hall, with a plaque written by Emperor Kangxi. Houses the Four Heavenly Kings and a Maitreya Buddha.
  - **Grand Hall of the Great Sage (大雄宝殿):** The main hall, with a 24.8m tall Sakyamuni Buddha statue—one of the largest wooden Buddha statues in China.
  - **Hall of the Medicine Buddha (药师殿):** Houses the Medicine Buddha and 12 guardian generals.
  - **Hall of the Five Hundred Arhats (五百罗汉堂):** A large hall with 500 life-sized arhat statues.
  - **Lingyin Temple Tea House:** A peaceful tea house in the temple complex, serving Longjing tea.
- **Pro tips:**
  - Buy incense at the entrance (¥3-5) if you want to make offerings.
  - Dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered) out of respect.
  - The temple is active—monks live and worship here. Be quiet and respectful.
  - Combine with a visit to nearby Feilai Peak caves and the surrounding hills.
  - There are many other temples in the area—Fayun Temple, Yongfu Temple, and Taoguang Temple are all worth a visit if you have time.

### Longjing Tea Plantations (龙井茶村)

Hangzhou is the home of Longjing (Dragon Well) tea, widely considered China's finest green tea. The tea plantations around Longjing Village (龙井村), Meijiawu (梅家坞), and Wengjiashan (翁家山) offer beautiful terraced tea fields, fresh tea, and a glimpse into traditional tea culture.

- **Tickets:** Free to visit the villages and plantations. Tea tasting costs ¥50-200 depending on the tea quality.
- **When to go:** **Late March to early April** for the spring tea harvest (the best and most expensive tea). The plantations are lush and green year-round, but spring is the most interesting time.
- **How to get there:** Bus 27 from West Lake area, or Didi/taxi (¥30-50, 20-30 minutes).
- **What to do:**
  - Walk through the tea plantations and terraced fields.
  - Visit a tea house and taste fresh Longjing tea (ask for "mingqian" tea—picked before Qingming festival in early April, the highest grade).
  - Watch a tea roasting demonstration (the tea is pan-roasted by hand, a skilled process).
  - Buy tea directly from farmers (be careful—quality varies, and tourist prices can be high. Buy from reputable shops or ask your hotel for recommendations).
  - Hike the trails through the tea hills (the trail from Longjing to Meijiawu is beautiful).
- **Pro tips:**
  - Longjing tea is expensive—genuine high-grade Longjing can cost ¥1,000-10,000+ per 500g. Be wary of "cheap Longjing"—it's likely not genuine or low quality.
  - The best tea is picked in early spring (before April 5). Summer and autumn tea is cheaper but less flavorful.
  - Meijiawu is less touristy than Longjing Village and has many good tea houses.
  - Combine with a visit to the China National Tea Museum (中国茶叶博物馆) nearby, which is free and excellent.

### Leifeng Pagoda (雷峰塔)

A iconic pagoda on the south shore of West Lake, made famous by the Legend of the White Snake (白蛇传). The original pagoda was built in 977 AD and collapsed in 1924. The current pagoda was rebuilt in 2002 with modern architecture (including an elevator) over the ruins of the original.

- **Tickets:** ¥40.
- **When to go:** Late afternoon for sunset (the "Leifeng Sunset" is one of the Ten Scenes of West Lake). The pagoda is illuminated at night.
- **Allow:** 1-1.5 hours.
- **What to see:**
  - The ruins of the original pagoda (preserved under glass in the basement).
  - The observation deck at the top for panoramic views of West Lake.
  - The bronze carvings depicting the Legend of the White Snake.
- **Pro tip:** The pagoda has an elevator, so it's accessible for all fitness levels. The views from the top are worth the entry fee.

### Xixi National Wetland Park (西溪国家湿地公园)

China's first national wetland park, located 5km west of West Lake. A 10km² urban wetland ecosystem with rivers, lakes, ponds, reed beds, traditional water towns, and villages. A peaceful escape from the city.

- **Tickets:** ¥80 (park entry) + ¥60 (boat ride) = ¥140 combined.
- **When to go:** Spring (cherry blossoms, reed shoots) or autumn (golden reeds, clear skies). Early morning for mist and fewer crowds.
- **Allow:** 3-4 hours.
- **What to do:**
  - Take a boat ride through the waterways and reed beds (the best way to experience the wetland).
  - Walk the boardwalks and trails through the wetland.
  - Visit the traditional water towns and villages (Xixi Village, Shentankou).
  - See the lotus ponds (summer) and reed beds (autumn).
  - Visit the Xixi Wetland Museum (free, inside the park).
- **Pro tip:** The wetland is large—wear comfortable shoes. Bring mosquito repellent in summer. The boat ride is highly recommended.

### Hefang Street (河坊街)

A restored historical street in Hangzhou's old town, near West Lake. Lined with traditional architecture, shops, tea houses, restaurants, and street food. A good place to experience Hangzhou's traditional culture and buy souvenirs.

- **Tickets:** Free.
- **When to go:** Evening for the lanterns and atmosphere. Can be crowded on weekends.
- **What to do:**
  - Browse the shops selling silk, tea, traditional medicine, and handicrafts.
  - Try street food—scallion pancakes, stinky tofu, sugar figurines, and Hangzhou snacks.
  - Visit the Hu Qing Yu Tang (胡庆余堂), a famous traditional Chinese medicine pharmacy (now a museum).
  - Watch traditional craftsmen at work—sugar blowing, paper cutting, and silk weaving.
- **Pro tip:** Hefang Street is touristy and prices are higher. Go for the atmosphere and a quick snack, but buy tea/silk from reputable shops elsewhere. Combine with a visit to the nearby Southern Song Imperial Street (南宋御街).

### China National Silk Museum (中国丝绸博物馆)

The largest silk museum in the world, located near West Lake. Hangzhou has been a silk production center for over 2,000 years, and this museum tells the full story of silk—from silkworm to finished product.

- **Tickets:** Free (reservation recommended via WeChat).
- **When to go:** Any time. Allow 1.5-2 hours.
- **What to see:**
  - The history of silk and the Silk Road.
  - Live silkworm demonstrations (in season).
  - Traditional silk weaving looms in action.
  - Ancient silk artifacts and modern silk fashion.
  - The "Silk Road" gallery with artifacts from across Asia.
- **Pro tip:** The museum has a great gift shop selling authentic Hangzhou silk at reasonable prices. Combine with a visit to the nearby China National Tea Museum if you have time.

## Where to Eat

Hangzhou cuisine (杭帮菜) is one of China's eight great culinary traditions. It's known for fresh, seasonal ingredients, delicate flavors, and beautiful presentation—not spicy, but subtly sweet and savory.

### Signature Dishes

- **West Lake Vinegar Fish (西湖醋鱼):** Hangzhou's most famous dish. Fresh grass carp cooked in a sweet and sour vinegar sauce. The fish is supposedly kept hungry for days to remove the muddy taste. ¥60-120.
  - **Where:** Lou Wai Lou (楼外楼) on Gushan Road—the most famous restaurant, but touristy and expensive. Try Zhi Wei Guan (知味观) or Grandma's Home (外婆家) for better value.
- **Longjing Shrimp (龙井虾仁):** Fresh river shrimp stir-fried with Longjing tea leaves. Delicate, fragrant, and beautiful. ¥80-150.
- **Beggar's Chicken (叫化鸡):** A whole chicken stuffed with herbs, wrapped in lotus leaves and clay, then roasted. The clay is cracked open at the table. Legend says it was invented by a beggar who stole a chicken and cooked it in clay to hide the smell. ¥80-150.
- **Dongpo Pork (东坡肉):** Named after the poet Su Dongpo, who was a Hangzhou governor. Chunks of pork belly braised in soy sauce, wine, and sugar until melt-in-your-mouth tender. ¥40-80.
- **Sister Song's Fish Soup (宋嫂鱼羹):** A thick, creamy fish soup with bamboo shoots, mushrooms, and egg. Named after a Song Dynasty woman who sold fish soup by the lake. ¥30-50.
- **Fried Eel Pieces (生爆鳝片):** Freshwater eel stir-fried with garlic, pepper, and soy sauce. Crispy on the outside, tender inside. ¥50-80.
- **Hangzhou Roast Duck (杭州酱鸭):** Duck marinated in soy sauce and spices, then roasted. Similar to Peking duck but with a darker, richer flavor. ¥50-80.

### Snacks & Street Food

- **Scallion Pancakes (葱包桧):** A Hangzhou breakfast staple—flatbread with scallions and youtiao (fried dough), pressed and grilled. ¥5-10.
- **Ding Sheng Cake (定胜糕):** A pink glutinous rice cake with red bean paste, traditionally eaten for good luck. ¥5-10.
- **Cat Ears (猫耳朵):** A noodle soup with small, cat-ear-shaped noodles, shrimp, and vegetables. ¥15-25.
- **West Lake Lotus Root Powder (西湖藕粉):** A sweet, translucent dessert made from lotus root starch, served with osmanthus syrup and nuts. ¥10-20.
- **Stinky Tofu (臭豆腐):** Fermented tofu, deep-fried and served with chili sauce. An acquired taste, but popular. ¥10-15.

### Restaurants

- **Lou Wai Lou (楼外楼):** The most famous Hangzhou restaurant, founded in 1848, located on Gushan Road by West Lake. Touristy but authentic. ¥150-300 per person.
- **Zhi Wei Guan (知味观):** Founded in 1913, famous for Hangzhou dim sum and snacks. Multiple locations. ¥50-100 per person.
- **Grandma's Home (外婆家):** Popular local chain serving Hangzhou home-style cooking at reasonable prices. Multiple locations. ¥60-100 per person. Expect to queue.
- **Green Tea Restaurant (绿茶餐厅):** Another popular chain, serving creative Hangzhou dishes in a beautiful setting. ¥60-100 per person.
- **Jin Sha (金沙厅):** Upscale Hangzhou cuisine at the Four Seasons Hotel. One of the best restaurants in the city. ¥500-1000 per person.
- **Hefang Street Food Stalls:** For cheap, quick eats—try the scallion pancakes, stinky tofu, and Ding Sheng cakes.

### Tea Houses

Hangzhou tea culture is serious business. Here are some great tea houses:
- **Tea House at Lou Wai Lou:** Traditional tea house by the lake, serving Longjing tea and snacks.
- **Hupao Spring Tea House (虎跑茶馆):** Located at Hupao Spring, famous for its spring water which is said to be the best for brewing Longjing tea.
- **Longjing Village Tea Houses:** Dozens of tea houses in the village, serving fresh tea with views of the plantations.
- **Taiji Tea House (太极茶道苑):** A traditional tea house on Hefang Street with tea ceremonies and performances.

## Where to Stay

### West Lake Area (西湖区)
- **Best for:** First-time visitors, scenery, convenience.
- **Price range:** ¥400-3000+/night.
- **Pros:** Walk to West Lake, restaurants, and attractions. Beautiful views.
- **Cons:** Can be expensive. Some areas are noisy on weekends.

### Wulin Square / City Center (下城区)
- **Best for:** Shopping, dining, business, transport.
- **Price range:** ¥300-1500/night.
- **Pros:** Central, well-connected (metro hub), lots of restaurants and shops. Close to the high-speed rail station.
- **Cons:** Farther from West Lake (10-15 min by metro/taxi). Less scenic.

### Xixi Wetland Area (西湖区西部)
- **Best for:** Nature, relaxation, quiet retreat.
- **Price range:** ¥500-2000/night.
- **Pros:** Quiet, surrounded by nature, close to Xixi Wetland. Many resorts and boutique hotels.
- **Cons:** Far from West Lake (20-30 min by taxi). Fewer restaurant options.

### Xianghu Lake Area (萧山区)
- **Best for:** Quiet alternative to West Lake, families.
- **Price range:** ¥300-1000/night.
- **Pros:** Quieter and less touristy than West Lake. Beautiful lake and parks.
- **Cons:** Far from West Lake and city center (30-45 min by metro/taxi).

### Budget Options
- **Hangzhou Youth Hostel (杭州青年旅舍):** Near West Lake, clean and social. ¥80-150/night for a dorm bed.
- **The North Line Hostel:** Near Lingyin Temple, quiet and scenic. ¥100-200/night.

### Luxury Options
- **Four Seasons Hotel Hangzhou at West Lake:** Luxury resort with villas, spa, and lake views. ¥2000-5000/night.
- **Amanfayun:** Ultra-luxury resort in a restored ancient village near Lingyin Temple. ¥5000-15000/night.
- **Park Hyatt Hangzhou:** Modern luxury hotel in the city center. ¥1500-3000/night.

## 2-Day Itinerary

### Day 1: West Lake & Temples
- **6:30 AM:** Sunrise at West Lake. Rent a bike and cycle the Su Causeway and Bai Causeway (few crowds, beautiful light).
- **8:30 AM:** Breakfast at a local restaurant (try scallion pancakes and soy milk).
- **9:30 AM:** Lingyin Temple + Feilai Peak (2-3 hours). Arrive early to beat crowds.
- **12:30 PM:** Lunch at a vegetarian restaurant near the temple OR return to West Lake area.
- **2:00 PM:** Leifeng Pagoda (1 hour) + walk along the south shore.
- **3:30 PM:** Boat ride on West Lake to Three Pools Mirroring the Moon (1 hour).
- **5:00 PM:** Walk the east shore (Orioles Singing in Willows) and watch sunset over the lake.
- **6:30 PM:** Dinner at a Hangzhou restaurant (try Lou Wai Lou, Grandma's Home, or Green Tea).
- **8:30 PM:** Evening walk around West Lake to see the illuminated pagodas and causeways. OR watch the "Impression West Lake" light show (if in season, ¥200-600).

### Day 2: Tea Culture & Old Town
- **8:00 AM:** Breakfast.
- **9:00 AM:** Longjing Tea Plantations (visit a village, walk the tea fields, taste fresh Longjing tea).
- **11:30 AM:** China National Tea Museum (free, 1 hour) OR China National Silk Museum.
- **1:00 PM:** Lunch at a tea house or local restaurant in the Longjing area.
- **2:30 PM:** Hefang Street + Southern Song Imperial Street (browse shops, try street food, visit Hu Qing Yu Tang medicine museum).
- **4:30 PM:** Xixi National Wetland Park (if time, 2-3 hours) OR relax at a tea house by West Lake.
- **7:00 PM:** Dinner at a local restaurant (try Hangzhou roast duck, Dongpo pork, and West Lake vinegar fish).
- **8:30 PM:** Final walk around West Lake or drinks at a lakeside bar.

## 4-Day Itinerary Extension

If you have 2 more days:

### Day 3: Xixi Wetland & Yellow Dragon Cave
- **Morning:** Xixi National Wetland Park (3-4 hours, including boat ride).
- **Lunch:** At a restaurant near the wetland.
- **Afternoon:** Yellow Dragon Sports Center area OR Wushan Hill (吴山) for panoramic views of the city and lake.
- **Evening:** Dinner + Hefang Street night market.

### Day 4: Day Trip
**Option A: Suzhou (苏州)**
- 45-60 minutes by high-speed train from Hangzhou East.
- Visit the classical gardens (Humble Administrator's Garden, Lingering Garden), Tiger Hill, and Pingjiang Road canal street.
- Return to Hangzhou in the evening.

**Option B: Huangshan (Yellow Mountain, 黄山)**
- 1.5-2 hours by high-speed train to Huangshan North, then 1 hour by bus to the mountain.
- One of China's most beautiful mountains—famous for pine trees, granite peaks, sea of clouds, and hot springs.
- Best done as an overnight trip (stay on the mountain for sunrise).

**Option C: Wuzhen Water Town (乌镇)**
- 1-1.5 hours by car/bus from Hangzhou.
- One of China's best-preserved water towns, with canals, stone bridges, and ancient houses.
- The night view is spectacular. Can be done as a day trip or overnight.

## Practical Tips

### Money & Payments
- Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted everywhere, including most restaurants, shops, ticket counters, and even street vendors.
- Carry ¥200-500 cash for small vendors, temples, and emergencies.
- ATMs are everywhere. Most accept international cards.

### Internet & VPN
- Google, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, YouTube are blocked.
- **Set up a VPN before arrival.** ExpressVPN, Astrill, and NordVPN work well.
- 4G/5G coverage is excellent throughout the city and surrounding areas.
- Most cafes, hotels, and restaurants offer free WiFi.

### Language
- English is not widely spoken outside major hotels, tourist attractions, and upscale restaurants.
- Download Pleco (Chinese dictionary) and Google Translate (offline pack) for menus and directions.
- Hangzhou dialect (Wu dialect) is spoken locally, but everyone understands and speaks standard Mandarin.

### Safety
- Hangzhou is very safe—one of the safest cities in China. Violent crime is extremely rare.
- Watch for pickpockets in crowded areas (West Lake, Hefang Street, train stations, bus stops).
- Be careful when cycling—share the road with cars, buses, and scooters. Wear a helmet if available.
- Use licensed tour operators and official ticket counters. Avoid "free tours" that lead to shopping scams (especially tea shop scams—be wary of strangers inviting you to a "tea ceremony").
- The lake is deep—swimming is not allowed. Be careful on the causeways and boat docks, especially with children.

### Health
- Hangzhou is humid—stay hydrated and use mosquito repellent, especially near the lake and wetland in summer.
- The food is generally safe—stick to busy restaurants with high turnover.
- Drink bottled or boiled water only.
- Bring sunscreen, a hat, and sunglasses—the sun can be strong even on cloudy days.
- Pharmacies are everywhere. For serious medical issues, Hangzhou has excellent hospitals (Zhejiang University School of Medicine Affiliated Hospitals).

### Tipping
- Tipping is not customary. No need to tip at restaurants, for taxis, or for guides.
- Some high-end hotels and tour guides may appreciate a small tip, but it's not expected.

### What to Pack
- Comfortable walking/cycling shoes (you'll walk and cycle a lot)
- Light, breathable clothing (humid climate)
- Rain jacket or umbrella (frequent rain, especially in spring and summer)
- Sunscreen, hat, and sunglasses
- Portable power bank (essential for navigation, photos, and payments)
- Universal adapter (Type A, C, I, 220V)
- Mosquito repellent
- Toilet paper (many public restrooms don't provide it)
- Hand sanitizer
- Swimsuit (if your hotel has a pool)
- A nice outfit for upscale restaurants (optional)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a visa to visit Hangzhou?
Most nationalities need a visa to visit China. However, citizens of 55+ countries can use the **144-hour visa-free transit policy** when transiting through Hangzhou (Xiaoshan Airport) to a third country. Check our [complete China visa guide](/posts/ultimate-guide-to-china-visa-for-tourists/) for details.

### How many days do I need in Hangzhou?
2 days is the minimum to see the highlights (West Lake, Lingyin Temple, tea plantations). 3-4 days allows for a more relaxed pace, day trips to Xixi Wetland, water towns, or Suzhou, and more time to enjoy the tea culture and lakeside lifestyle. Many travelers combine Hangzhou with Shanghai (45 min by train) or Suzhou (1 hour by train).

### Is West Lake really worth the hype?
Yes, absolutely. West Lake is one of China's most beautiful and culturally significant sites. The combination of natural beauty (misty waters, willow-lined shores, pagoda-dotted hills) and cultural heritage (1,000+ years of poetry, painting, and legend) makes it truly special. The key is to visit at the right time—early morning or late afternoon, on a weekday, in spring or autumn. Go at noon on a weekend in summer and you'll find it crowded and hot, but go at sunrise on a spring weekday and you'll understand why it's called "Paradise on Earth."

### What's the best way to see West Lake?
**Rent a bike and cycle around at sunrise.** This is the classic Hangzhou experience and the best way to see the lake. The full loop is ~15km and takes 2-3 hours. Start at the Su Causeway, cycle to the Bai Causeway, and continue around the north and east shores. Stop frequently for photos and to enjoy the views. If cycling isn't your thing, take a boat cruise to the Three Pools island, or walk the causeways (they're pedestrian-only).

### Is Longjing tea worth buying in Hangzhou?
Yes, if you buy from a reputable source and know what you're doing. Genuine high-grade Longjing is one of the world's finest teas—delicate, fragrant, and subtly sweet. But it's expensive (¥1,000-10,000+ per 500g for top grades) and there's a lot of fake/low-quality tea sold to tourists. Tips: Buy from reputable tea shops (not street stalls), ask for "mingqian" tea (picked before April 5), taste before you buy, and don't be afraid to ask questions. The China National Tea Museum shop is a safe bet. If you're not a tea connoisseur, a mid-grade tea (¥200-500/500g) is still excellent and much more affordable.

### Is Hangzhou safe for solo travelers?
Yes, Hangzhou is one of the safest cities in China for solo travelers, including women. The city is wealthy, well-policed, and tourist-friendly. Violent crime is extremely rare. Use common sense with pickpockets in crowded areas, be careful when cycling, and use licensed operators. The locals are generally friendly and helpful. Hangzhou is also a great city for solo travelers because it's easy to get around, has a relaxed atmosphere, and there's plenty to do alone (cycling, tea tasting, temple visits).

### What should I eat in Hangzhou?
Must-try dishes: West Lake vinegar fish (西湖醋鱼), Longjing shrimp (龙井虾仁), Beggar's chicken (叫化鸡), Dongpo pork (东坡肉), Sister Song's fish soup (宋嫂鱼羹), and Hangzhou roast duck (杭州酱鸭). For snacks, try scallion pancakes (葱包桧), Ding Sheng cake (定胜糕), cat ear noodles (猫耳朵), and West Lake lotus root powder (西湖藕粉). Wash it all down with a cup of fresh Longjing tea.

### Can I visit Hangzhou as a day trip from Shanghai?
Yes, you can visit Hangzhou as a day trip from Shanghai (45-60 minutes by high-speed train, ¥73-250 one way). But it's not recommended—there's too much to see and do in a single day, and the relaxed lakeside lifestyle is part of the appeal. If you're short on time, at least stay one night to experience West Lake at sunrise and in the evening. A 2-day trip is ideal.

### Is Hangzhou too touristy?
Hangzhou can be touristy, especially West Lake on weekends and holidays. But it's easy to escape the crowds—go early in the morning, visit on a weekday, explore the less-visited west and south shores of the lake, or venture into the tea hills and surrounding villages. The city is large and has many quiet, beautiful spots away from the main tourist areas. Go in the off-season (winter) for even fewer crowds.

### Is Hangzhou worth visiting?
Absolutely. Hangzhou is one of China's most beautiful and livable cities, with a perfect blend of natural beauty, cultural heritage, and modern comfort. Whether you're cycling around West Lake at sunrise, sipping Longjing tea in a mountain village, exploring ancient Buddhist temples, or eating your way through the city's excellent restaurants, Hangzhou offers a uniquely wonderful experience. It's easy to see why it's been called "Paradise on Earth" for over 1,000 years.
