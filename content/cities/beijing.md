Beijing is where China's imperial past collides head-on with its futuristic present. You'll cycle past 600-year-old palace walls in the morning and sip craft beer in a restored hutong courtyard by night. It's chaotic, grand, and absolutely unmissable.

As the capital of China for over 800 years, Beijing packs more UNESCO World Heritage Sites into its metropolitan area than almost any other city on Earth. From the sweeping ramparts of the Great Wall to the intricate courtyards of the Forbidden City, every corner tells a story.

![The Great Wall of China at Mutianyu](/img/china-dest/beijing/2026-06-19-the-history-and-culture-of-the-great-wall-beyond-the-tourist-trail-guide.webp)

## Best Time to Visit Beijing

Beijing has four distinct seasons, and the best time to visit depends on what you want:

- **Late September to early November (Autumn):** The best time. Clear skies, comfortable temperatures (15-25°C/59-77°F), and the city's famous ginkgo trees turn golden. Book early—this is peak season.
- **March to May (Spring):** Pleasant weather but occasional sandstorms. Cherry blossoms and peach blossoms bloom in late March/early April.
- **June to August (Summer):** Hot (30-35°C/86-95°F) and humid. Summer Palace and other gardens are lush, but prepare for heat and occasional thunderstorms.
- **December to February (Winter):** Cold (-5 to 5°C/23-41°F) but very few tourists. The Great Wall covered in snow is spectacular, and hotel prices are at their lowest.

**Avoid:** Chinese New Year (late January/early February) when many attractions close or are extremely crowded, and the first week of October (National Day holiday) when domestic tourism peaks.

## Getting There & Around

### Airports

- **PEK (Capital International Airport):** Older, closer to city center (25km/16mi northeast). Airport Express train to Dongzhimen in 30 minutes (¥25). Most international flights arrive here.
- **PKX (Daxing International Airport):** Newer, massive, designed by Zaha Hadid. 46km/29mi south of city center. Airport Express connects to Caoqiao subway station in 19 minutes (¥35). Primarily serves domestic and some international flights.

**Pro tip:** Check which airport your flight uses before booking accommodation—they're 70km apart!

### Metro

The Beijing Subway is the world's longest subway system (over 700km/435mi) and the cheapest way to get around. Fares start at ¥3 and max out at ¥10.

- **Payment:** Use Alipay or WeChat Pay transit QR code (set up before arrival), or buy a Beijing Transit Card (¥20 deposit) at any station.
- **Hours:** Approximately 5:30 AM to 11:00 PM (varies by line).
- **Avoid rush hours:** 7-9 AM and 5-8 PM unless you enjoy human sardine situations. Major stations like Xidan, Guomao, and Dongzhimen get extremely crowded.

### Didi (Ride-Hailing)

Didi is China's Uber equivalent and is cheap and reliable. A typical cross-city ride costs ¥30-60.

- **Download the app** before arrival and set up payment with Alipay/WeChat.
- **Have your hotel name written in Chinese** to show the driver.
- **Airport rides:** Pre-book via the app for fixed prices, or use the official taxi queue (expect ¥100-150 to city center).

### Bicycle

Beijing is surprisingly bike-friendly, with dedicated bike lanes on most major roads. Mobike and Meituan bike-sharing are everywhere (¥1.5 per 30 minutes). The flat terrain around the Forbidden City and hutong areas makes cycling a joy.

## Must-See Sights

### The Forbidden City (Palace Museum)

![Forbidden City Beijing](/img/china-dest/beijing/7-day-china-first-timer-itinerary-beijing-xian-shanghai.jpg)

The largest palace complex in the world, home to 24 emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties. 980 buildings spread across 72 hectares—you could spend an entire day here and still not see everything.

**Tickets:** ¥60 (peak season April-October), ¥40 (off-season November-March). Special exhibitions (Treasure Gallery, Clock Gallery) cost an additional ¥10 each.

**Booking:** Book tickets **7 days in advance** on the official WeChat mini-program "故宫博物院" or website (dpm.org.cn). Limited to 80,000 visitors daily and they sell out fast, especially on weekends and holidays.

**When to go:** Arrive at 8:30 AM when doors open. The first two hours are the least crowded. Avoid Mondays (closed) and Chinese holidays.

**How to visit:** Enter from the south (Meridian Gate), walk north through the central axis, and exit through the Gate of Divine Prowess. Allow **3-4 hours minimum**. Don't miss:
- Hall of Supreme Harmony (the largest wooden structure in China)
- Imperial Garden (north end)
- Treasure Gallery (need separate ticket)
- Clock Gallery (need separate ticket)

**Pro tip:** Wear comfortable shoes—you'll walk 5-8km on stone paving. Bring water (only one or two shops inside).

### Great Wall

![Great Wall of China](/img/china-dest/beijing/beijing-great-wall-hiking-guide.jpg)

No visit to Beijing is complete without walking the Great Wall. But not all sections are equal. Skip Badaling (the tourist circus with cable cars and crowds) and go to one of these instead:

#### Mutianyu
- **Best for:** First-time visitors, families, anyone who wants a well-restored section with facilities.
- **Distance:** 73km/45mi northeast, 1.5-2 hours by car.
- **Tickets:** ¥45 entrance + ¥100 round-trip cable car or ¥120 toboggan slide down (yes, really).
- **Why go:** Restored but not overcrowded, beautiful scenery with watchtowers, and the toboggan slide is genuinely fun.
- **How to get there:** Book a private car/driver (¥400-600 round trip), take the 916 fast bus from Dongzhimen to Huairou then transfer, or join a small-group tour.

#### Jinshanling
- **Best for:** Hikers, photographers, anyone who wants a wilder experience.
- **Distance:** 130km/81mi northeast, 2-2.5 hours by car.
- **Tickets:** ¥65 entrance. Cable car available (¥80 round trip).
- **Why go:** Partially unrestored, far fewer crowds, and the 10km hike between Jinshanling and Simatai is one of the best Great Wall walks.
- **How to get there:** Private car or tour. Public transport is complicated.

#### Jiankou (Unrestored)
- **Best for:** Experienced hikers only.
- **Distance:** 73km/45mi northeast.
- **Tickets:** Technically ¥20, but this is a wild, unrestored section.
- **Warning:** Steep, crumbling, and potentially dangerous. Several tourists have died here. Only go with an experienced guide and proper hiking gear.

**Great Wall tips:**
- Go on a weekday if possible.
- Start early (before 9 AM) to beat crowds and heat.
- Bring plenty of water and snacks—prices on the wall are 3-5x normal.
- Wear sturdy shoes with good grip.
- Check the weather—visibility can be poor on smoggy days.

### Temple of Heaven

A masterpiece of Ming dynasty architecture, built in 1420 as the stage where emperors performed annual ceremonies to pray for good harvests.

**Tickets:** ¥15 (park only) or ¥34 (includes Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests, Imperial Vault of Heaven, and Circular Mound Altar).

**When to go:** **Sunrise (6-7 AM)** is magical—locals gather to do tai chi, play erhu, practice water calligraphy, and sing opera. The Echo Wall (where a whisper travels 60m) and Circular Mound Altar are genuinely impressive.

**Allow:** 2-3 hours. The park is large (273 hectares) and beautiful to walk through.

### Summer Palace

The largest and best-preserved royal garden in China, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Empress Dowager Cixi famously embezzled navy funds to build her summer retreat here.

**Tickets:** ¥30 (park only) or ¥60 (includes all halls and gardens). Boat rides on Kunming Lake cost ¥50-100.

**When to go:** Morning or late afternoon. The park is huge (290 hectares)—allow half a day. Don't miss:
- Long Corridor (728m painted with 14,000 scenes from Chinese mythology)
- Marble Boat (Cixi's infamous "navy")
- Seventeen-Arch Bridge
- Tower of Buddhist Incense (panoramic views over the lake)

### Hutongs

These ancient alleyways are Beijing's soul. Once numbering over 6,000, only about 1,000 remain after decades of demolition.

- **Nanluoguxiang:** The most famous hutong, full of shops, cafes, and crowds. Go early morning to see it without the tourist hordes.
- **Wudaoying Hutong:** Trendier, less crowded, with boutique shops and good cafes.
- **Guozijian Street:** Home to the Imperial Academy and Confucius Temple, tree-lined and peaceful.
- **Shichahai area:** Lakeside hutongs with bars, restaurants, and rickshaw tours. Best visited at sunset.

**How to explore:** Walk or rent a bicycle. Rickshaw tours (¥150-300 per hour) are available but can be touristy. Get lost in the smaller lanes—that's where you'll find real Beijing life.

## Where to Eat

Beijing's food scene is as diverse as its history. From imperial cuisine to street food, here's what to eat and where:

### Peking Duck

The city's signature dish. Crispy skin, tender meat, wrapped in thin pancakes with scallion, cucumber, and sweet bean sauce.

- **Siji Minfu (四季民福):** Locals' favorite. Multiple locations. The Forbidden City branch has views of the palace walls. Expect to queue. ¥150-200 per person.
- **Jingzun Peking Duck (京尊烤鸭):** Good value, less touristy. ¥120-180 per person.
- **Da Dong (大董):** Upscale, creative presentation. ¥300-500 per person.
- **Quanjude (全聚德):** The most famous name (founded 1864), but considered overpriced and touristy by locals.

**How to order:** One whole duck serves 2-3 people. Most restaurants serve it in 3 courses: sliced duck with pancakes, duck soup, and stir-fried duck meat.

### Street Food & Local Eats

- **Niujie (牛街):** The Muslim Quarter. Best for lamb skewers (羊肉串), beef pancakes, and Islamic pastries. Go hungry.
- **Guijie (簋街):** "Ghost Street"—24-hour restaurant strip famous for spicy crayfish (麻辣小龙虾). Best at night.
- **Wangfujing Snack Street:** Touristy and overpriced, but worth a visit for the exotic food stalls (scorpions, seahorses, starfish on sticks).
- **Jianbing (煎饼):** Chinese crepe with egg, crispy cracker, and sauce. ¥6-10 from any street cart. The ultimate Beijing breakfast.
- **Zhajiangmian (炸酱面):** Noodles with fried soybean paste. A Beijing classic. Try at any local noodle shop.
- **Douzhi (豆汁):** Fermented mung bean drink. An acquired taste—locals love it, most tourists hate it. Try it if you're brave.

### Hotpot

Beijing-style hotpot (涮羊肉) features thinly sliced lamb cooked in clear broth, dipped in sesame sauce.

- **Haidilao (海底捞):** The famous chain with excellent service (free manicures, snacks while waiting). Multiple locations. ¥150-250 per person.
- **Jubaoyuan (聚宝源):** Local favorite in Niujie. Authentic Muslim-style hotpot. Expect queues. ¥120-180 per person.

## Where to Stay

Beijing is huge—where you stay matters. The best areas for tourists are:

### Wangfujing / Dongcheng (City Center)
- **Best for:** First-time visitors, proximity to Forbidden City and Tiananmen.
- **Price range:** ¥300-800/night mid-range.
- **Pros:** Walk to major attractions, lots of restaurants and shops, good metro access.
- **Cons:** Can be noisy and crowded.

### Qianmen / Dashilan (South of Tiananmen)
- **Best for:** Budget travelers, traditional Beijing atmosphere.
- **Price range:** ¥150-500/night.
- **Pros:** Close to Forbidden City, hutong area, cheaper than Wangfujing.
- **Cons:** Some areas are run down, fewer international hotels.

### Sanlitun / Chaoyang (Embassy District)
- **Best for:** Nightlife, shopping, international restaurants.
- **Price range:** ¥500-1500/night.
- **Pros:** Trendy area, English widely spoken, close to embassies and CBD.
- **Cons:** Far from historical attractions (30-45 min by metro).

### Hutong Boutique Hotels
- **Best for:** Unique experience, couples, design lovers.
- **Price range:** ¥600-2000/night.
- **Top picks:**
  - **Hotel Cote Cour Beijing:** Restored courtyard in Dongcheng. Beautiful, intimate.
  - **The Orchid Hotel:** Boutique hotel in a hutong near Drum Tower.
  - **Wudadao No. 8:** Design-forward courtyard hotel.

### Budget Options
- **Leo Hostel:** Near Qianmen, clean and social. ¥100-200/night for a dorm bed.
- **Peking Youth Hostel:** Near Qianmen, good location and facilities. ¥80-150/night.

- **8:30 AM:** Forbidden City (book ahead!)
- **12:30 PM:** Lunch at a nearby restaurant (try Siji Minfu for duck)
- **2:00 PM:** Jingshan Park (climb to the top for the best panoramic view of the Forbidden City)
- **3:30 PM:** Beihai Park (stroll through the imperial garden)
- **5:30 PM:** Nanluoguxiang hutong area (explore, shop, snack)
- **7:30 PM:** Dinner in the hutong area

### Day 2: Great Wall & Olympic Park
- **7:00 AM:** Depart for Mutianyu Great Wall (private car or tour)
- **9:00 AM - 1:00 PM:** Hike the Great Wall (take cable car up, toboggan down)
- **1:30 PM:** Lunch at a restaurant near the wall
- **4:00 PM:** Return to city
- **6:00 PM:** Olympic Park (see Bird's Nest and Water Cube lit up at night)
- **8:00 PM:** Dinner in Sanlitun or Guijie

### Day 3: Temples, Markets & Local Life
- **7:00 AM:** Temple of Heaven (see locals doing morning exercises)
- **10:00 AM:** Summer Palace (half day)
- **2:00 PM:** Lunch near Summer Palace
- **4:00 PM:** 798 Art District (contemporary art galleries, cafes, street art) OR Panjiayuan Antique Market (weekends only)
- **7:00 PM:** Dinner at Niujie (Muslim Quarter) or hotpot
- **9:00 PM:** Shichahai lakeside walk (bars, lanterns, rickshaws)

## Practical Tips

### Money & Payments
- Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted everywhere. Set them up before arrival with your passport and international credit card.
- Carry some cash (¥500-1000) for small vendors, temples, and emergencies.
- ATMs are widely available. Bank of China, ICBC, and Construction Bank accept international cards.

### Internet & VPN
- Google, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, YouTube, and most Western apps are blocked in China.
- **Download and set up a VPN before arrival** (ExpressVPN, Astrill, or NordVPN work well). Test it before you go.
- Alternatively, buy a local eSIM with VPN included (Airalo offers China eSIMs).
- Baidu Maps works for navigation (in Chinese), or use Google Maps with VPN.

### Air Quality
- Beijing's air quality varies. Check the AQI (Air Quality Index) on apps like AirVisual.
- If AQI > 150, wear a N95 mask outdoors and avoid strenuous exercise.
- Good air days (AQI < 50) are common in autumn and after rain.

### Safety
- Beijing is generally very safe for tourists. Violent crime is rare.
- Watch out for pickpockets in crowded areas (subway, Wangfujing, Great Wall).
- Avoid the "art student" scam near Wangfujing—people offering to take you to an art gallery are running a scam.
- Keep your passport and valuables secure.

### Language
- English is not widely spoken outside major hotels and tourist areas.
- Download Google Translate (offline Chinese pack) or Pleco (Chinese dictionary app) before arrival.
- Learn a few basic phrases: 你好 (nǐ hǎo - hello), 谢谢 (xiè xiè - thank you), 多少钱 (duō shǎo qián - how much), 不要辣 (bú yào là - no spice).

### Tipping
- Tipping is not customary in China. Taxi drivers, restaurant staff, and hotel workers do not expect tips.
- Some high-end hotels and tour guides may appreciate a small tip, but it's not required.

### What to Pack
- Comfortable walking shoes (you'll walk 10,000+ steps per day)
- Light layers (temperatures vary a lot between morning and evening)
- N95 mask (for poor air quality days)
- Portable power bank (essential for navigation and payments)
- Universal power adapter (China uses Type A, C, and I plugs, 220V)
- Travel umbrella (sudden rain is common in summer)
- Toilet paper (many public restrooms don't provide it)
- Hand sanitizer

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a visa to visit Beijing?
Most nationalities need a visa to visit China. However, citizens of 55+ countries can use the **144-hour visa-free transit policy** if they're transiting through Beijing to a third country. Check our [complete China visa guide](/posts/ultimate-guide-to-china-visa-for-tourists/) for details.

### How many days do I need in Beijing?
3-4 days is the minimum to see the highlights (Forbidden City, Great Wall, Temple of Heaven, Summer Palace). 5-7 days allows for a more relaxed pace and day trips.

### Is Beijing safe for solo travelers?
Yes, Beijing is very safe for solo travelers, including women. The city is well-lit, public transport is safe, and violent crime is rare. Use common sense with pickpockets and scams.

### Can I drink tap water in Beijing?
No. Tap water is not drinkable. Drink bottled water (¥2-5 per bottle) or boiled water. Most hotels provide electric kettles.

### How do I get from the airport to the city?
- **Airport Express train:** ¥25-35, 20-30 minutes. Cheapest and fastest option.
- **Taxi:** ¥100-150 to city center, 30-60 minutes depending on traffic.
- **Didi:** ¥80-120, book via app.
- **Airport bus:** ¥30-40, multiple routes, but slower (1-1.5 hours).

### What's the best Great Wall section for first-timers?
**Mutianyu** is the best choice for first-timers. It's well-restored, has cable cars and a toboggan slide, and is much less crowded than Badaling.

### Do I need to book tickets in advance?
**Yes!** The Forbidden City requires booking 7 days in advance and often sells out. The National Museum, Summer Palace, and Temple of Heaven also recommend advance booking via their WeChat mini-programs.

### Is Beijing expensive?
Beijing can be very affordable or very expensive, depending on your travel style. Budget travelers can spend ¥300-500/day (hostel, street food, metro). Mid-range: ¥800-1500/day. Luxury: ¥3000+/day.

### Can I use my credit card in Beijing?
International credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are accepted at major hotels, upscale restaurants, and some shopping malls. But most local shops, restaurants, and attractions only accept Alipay, WeChat Pay, or cash. Set up mobile payment before arrival.
