## China Visa Requirements

### Types of Visas
First things first, let's talk about visa requirements. As an international traveler from Europe, you'll need to apply for a China tourist visa (L visa) before you jet off. The process can seem a bit like a maze at first, but don't worry, I'm here to be your guide through it all.

There are several types of China visas available, but the most common for tourists is the L visa. This bad boy allows you to stay in China for up to 90 days for tourism purposes. It's like a golden ticket to explore all the amazing things this country has to offer. If you plan to stay longer, you'll need to apply for a different type of visa. Maybe you'll fall in love with China so much that you'll want to extend your stay and soak up even more culture, food, and experiences.

### Application Process
To apply for a China tourist visa, you'll need to fill out an online application form and submit it along with the required documents. The required documents may vary depending on your nationality and the type of visa you're applying for, but generally include:
- A valid passport with at least 6 months remaining before expiration. This is your ticket to getting into China, so make sure it's in tip-top shape.
- A completed visa application form. Fill it out carefully, double-checking all the information to avoid any delays.
- A passport-sized photo. Make sure it meets all the requirements, like the right size and background color.
- Proof of travel arrangements, such as flight tickets or hotel reservations. This shows that you have a plan and are ready to hit the ground running once you arrive.
- Proof of sufficient funds to cover your stay in China, such as bank statements or credit card statements. You don't want to run out of money while you're there, so this is an important one.

Once you've submitted your application and documents, you'll need to schedule an appointment at the nearest Chinese embassy or consulate for a visa interview. The interview is usually very brief and focuses on your travel plans and reasons for visiting China. Just be honest and confident, and you'll be fine.

### Visa Fees
The visa fee for a China tourist visa can vary depending on your nationality and the type of visa you're applying for. It's a small price to pay for the amazing experiences you'll have in China. Make sure to check the official website of the Chinese embassy or consulate in your country for the most up-to-date information on visa fees.

## Transportation in China

### Getting Around in Cities
When it comes to getting around in Chinese cities, you have several options. The subway is usually the most convenient and efficient way to travel, especially in big cities like Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangzhou. It's clean, fast, and easy to use. Just make sure to buy a subway card or use your mobile payment app to get on the train.

Taxis are also available, but they can be a bit more expensive, especially during peak traffic hours. If you do take a taxi, make sure the driver uses the meter and doesn't try to overcharge you. You can also try using ride-sharing apps like Didi, which are becoming popular in China.

Buses are another option, but they can be a bit more crowded and slower than the subway. However, they can be a great way to see the city and get a feel for the local culture. Just make sure to have exact change or a bus card.

### Traveling Between Cities
If you want to travel between cities, your a popular bet is to take the high-speed rail. China has an extensive high-speed rail network that connects major cities across the country. It's fast, comfortable, and convenient. You can book your tickets online or at the train station. Just make sure to arrive at the station early and bring your passport or other valid identification.

If you're on a budget, you can also take the regular train. However, it can be a bit slower and less comfortable than the high-speed rail. You can also consider flying, but it can be more expensive, especially if you book your tickets last minute.

### Chinese Cuisine
One of the highlights of any trip to China is the food. Chinese cuisine is one of the most diverse and delicious in the world, with something to suit every taste and budget. From the spicy and flavorful Sichuan cuisine to the sweet and savory Cantonese cuisine, there's no shortage of amazing dishes to try.

Some of the must-try dishes in China include Peking duck, dumplings, noodles, stir-fry, and hot pot. Peking duck is a famous Chinese dish that consists of crispy roasted duck skin served with thin pancakes, scallions, and a special sauce. Dumplings are another popular dish that can be filled with a variety of ingredients, such as pork, beef, vegetables, or seafood. Noodles are a staple of Chinese cuisine and can be served in a variety of ways, such as in a soup, with a sauce, or with vegetables and meat. Stir-fry is a quick and easy way to cook vegetables and meat, and it's usually served with rice or noodles. Hot pot is a popular Chinese dish that consists of a simmering pot of broth filled with a variety of ingredients, such as meat, vegetables, seafood, and noodles.

### Street Food
In addition to the traditional Chinese dishes, you also have to try the street food. Street food is a big part of Chinese culture, and it's a great way to sample a variety of local specialties. Some of the must-try street foods in China include baozi (steamed buns), jianbing (Chinese crepes), roujiamo (Chinese hamburgers), and tanghulu (candied fruit).

Baozi are steamed buns that can be filled with a variety of ingredients, such as pork, beef, vegetables, or seafood. Jianbing are Chinese crepes that are usually filled with eggs, scallions, and a special sauce. Roujiamo are Chinese hamburgers that consist of a pita bread filled with stewed meat. Tanghulu are candied fruit that is usually made with hawthorn berries, but can also be made with other fruits, such as apples, oranges, or bananas.

### Local Specialties
Each region in China has its own unique local specialties that you have to try. For example, in Sichuan, you have to try the spicy hot pot, mapo tofu, and kung pao chicken. In Guangdong, you have to try the dim sum, roast duck, and stir-fry. In Shandong, you have to try the Shandong cuisine, which is known for its use of seafood, vegetables, and noodles.

## Safety in China

### General Safety Tips
China is generally a very safe country to travel to, but it's often important to take some precautions to ensure your safety. Here are some general safety tips to keep in mind:
- Be aware of your surroundings and avoid walking alone at night, especially in unfamiliar areas.
- Keep your valuables safe and avoid carrying large amounts of cash or wearing expensive jewelry.
- Use caution when using ATMs or credit cards, and make sure to cover the keypad when entering your PIN.
- Follow the local laws and customs, and respect the local culture and traditions.
- If you have any problems or emergencies, contact the local police or your embassy or consulate.

### Scams and Rip-offs
Like any other country, China has its fair share of scams and rip-offs. Here are some common scams and rip-offs to watch out for:
- The "friendly stranger" scam: This is when a stranger approaches you and offers to help you with something, such as finding a hotel or getting a taxi. However, they may be trying to rob you or sell you something.
- The "fake police" scam: This is when someone dressed as a police officer approaches you and asks to see your passport or other identification. However, they may be trying to rob you or extort money from you.
- The "tea house" scam: This is when you are invited to a tea house by a stranger and are then pressured to buy expensive tea or other products.
- The "taxi" scam: This is when a taxi driver tries to overcharge you or takes you on a longer route than necessary.

To avoid these scams and rip-offs, it's important to be aware of your surroundings, trust your instincts, and avoid dealing with strangers who seem too good to be true.
