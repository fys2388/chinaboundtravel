## Visa and Documents
### Understanding the Visa Process
First things first, let's delve into the world of visas. US passport holders are eligible for 144 - hour visa-free transit in China, which is a great option if you're just passing through. However, if you're planning to stay longer, you'll need to apply for the appropriate visa. This process can be a bit daunting, but it's crucial to have all your documents in order well in advance.

Many first-time visitors find their first visa application overwhelming — countless forms to fill out, a long list of requirements to meet, and a general sense of confusion. But once you navigate through it successfully, you're on your way to an amazing Chinese adventure.

### Keeping Copies of Important Documents
Actionable tip: Keep a copy of all your important documents (passport, visa, travel itinerary) in a separate place from the originals. This can be an absolute lifesaver if something gets lost or stolen. Many travelers share stories of having their wallets snatched on busy streets — luckily, having copies of passports and other important documents stashed away in a hotel safe can save the day. This way, you'll still have access to the information you need to prove your identity and travel plans.

For more in-depth information on visas, check out [Ultimate Guide to China Visa for Tourists 2026](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/ultimate-guide-to-china-visa-for-tourists/) and [144 - Hour Visa-Free Transit in China-The Complete 2026 Guide](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/144-hour-visa-free-transit-guide/).

## Clothing
### Adapting to China's Diverse Climate
The climate in China can vary greatly depending on where you're going and the time of year. In Chengdu, for example, it can be quite humid in the summer and cold and damp in the winter. So, packing layers is key. Think of it as packing for a generally not recommended-ending game of 'temperature roulette'.

In the summer, lightweight, breathable clothes are an absolute must. You'll be doing a lot of exploring, and trust us, blisters are not your friend. So, don't forget a good pair of comfortable walking shoes. Many travelers make the mistake of wearing new shoes on a long day of sightseeing in Beijing, and they pay the price with painful blisters.

### Winter Wear in China
In the winter, bring warm hats, scarves, and thick coats. The cold in China can be quite biting, especially in the northern regions. Many first-time visitors to Chengdu think they can get away with a light jacket in winter — big mistake! They quickly realize they need to invest in some proper winter gear.

Actionable tip: If you're traveling to areas like Zhangjiajie in the winter, consider bringing thermal underwear. It can keep you warm without adding too much bulk to your luggage. You can read more about Zhangjiajie in [Zhangjiajie Avatar Mountains: Complete Guide to China's Most Spectacular Park](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/zhangjiajie-avatar-mountains-complete-guide-to-chinas-most-spectacular-park/).

## Electronics
### Staying Connected
When it comes to electronics, your smartphone is going to be your most important companion. However, make sure you have a plan for staying connected. You can either get a local SIM card or use an eSIM. Many travelers prefer using an eSIM as it's more convenient and doesn't require swapping out physical cards.

Actionable tip: Download useful apps before you arrive in China. Apps like WeChat are essential for communication and can also be used for mobile payments. Check out [How to Use WeChat Pay as a Foreigner](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/how-to-use-wechat-pay-as-a-foreigner/) for more details.

### Power Adaptors
Don't forget to bring a power adaptor. China uses a different type of electrical outlet, and without the right adaptor, your electronics won't be able to charge. Many travelers share stories of arriving in China without one and having to make a panicked trip to a local electronics store to buy one.

## Other Items to Consider
### Money and Payments
China is a cash-less society to a large extent. Most places accept mobile payments like Alipay and WeChat Pay. However, it's still a good idea to have some cash on hand, especially in smaller towns or when dealing with street vendors. You can read more about using mobile payments in [2026 Ultimate Guide to Using Alipay & WeChat Pay for Foreigners (No Chinese Bank Account Needed)](https://www.chinaboundtravel.com/posts/alipay-wechat-pay-foreigners-guide/). For Alipay specifically, see our dedicated [Alipay for Foreigners guide](/posts/alipay-for-foreigners-guide/).

Actionable tip: If you plan to use your credit card, make sure it's accepted in China. Some cards may not work, so it's a good idea to check with your bank before you travel.

### Gifts
If you're planning to visit someone's home or want to give gifts to locals, it's a nice gesture. Chinese people appreciate gifts, especially ones that are unique or have a personal touch. You can consider bringing some Californian specialties like wine or almonds.

## What to Leave at Home
### Heavy Books and Magazines
While it might be tempting to bring your entire library with you, it's a popular to leave heavy books and magazines at home. You can often buy books or e-books in China, or use your e-reader.

### Excessive Jewelry
China is a relatively safe country, but it's still not a good idea to wear excessive jewelry. It can attract unwanted attention.

### Large Quantities of Medication
If you need to take medication, bring only the amount you need for your trip. China has strict regulations regarding the import of medications, and bringing too much can cause problems.

### Building a Practical Packing List

A good China packing list balances practicality with season and itinerary. Universal essentials include a universal adapter, a power bank, comfortable walking shoes, and weather-appropriate layers. Carry only what you can manage easily, since you'll move between cities and use public transport.

If your trip involves multiple climate zones, check the forecast for each destination.

---

### What to Bring and What to Leave

- Bring: adapter, power bank, comfortable shoes, essential medications.
- Bring: a VPN and eSIM setup if you need familiar web services.
- Leave: bulky electronics and items easily bought on arrival.
- Consider: a reusable water bottle and a small daypack.
- Check: airline baggage limits before you fly.

### Final Pre-Departure Checks

Confirm your documents, confirm your payment setup works, and check whether your hotel can assist with common needs. Share your itinerary with someone you trust, and note your embassy's contact details. A little preparation before departure makes the first days in China much smoother.
