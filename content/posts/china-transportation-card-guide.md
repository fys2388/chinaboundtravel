Short answer: **no, not strictly** -- but a transportation card can make city travel smoother.

In most Chinese cities, the metro gate can be passed by scanning a QR code from Alipay or WeChat Pay. That means many tourists complete an entire trip without ever buying a card. However, physical transit cards still matter in specific situations:

- **Phone battery or signal problems**: QR payment needs a working phone and internet connection. A physical card works offline.
- **Multi-city trips**: some physical IC cards participate in China's national transit-card interconnect program, so one card can cover several cities.
- **Local habit and convenience**: cards are faster at the gate than opening an app, and they avoid any confusion about which payment method the local metro accepts.

The rest of this guide covers the three main options, city-by-city examples, and a clear recommendation for tourists.

### 1. Metro IC Cards (Physical Transit Cards)

Physical transit cards are plastic or chip cards sold at metro stations. You pay a deposit (typically small and refundable) and load balance in cash or by app. Cards are tapped at the gate and can also be used on many city buses. Because the card stores value locally, it keeps working even when your phone has no signal.

For foreign travelers, the main advantages are reliability and not needing a Chinese payment setup at the station. The main disadvantage is that each city issues its own card, so coverage depends on the national interconnect program.

### 2. Transit Apps and Digital Payments

City metro operators publish official apps for transit payment and route planning -- for example, the Beijing and Shanghai metro apps. These apps let you pay at the gate with a QR code and show live train positions. Many also provide English interfaces for route planning.

The catch is that most transit apps expect a Chinese payment method. For most foreign tourists, the more practical route is the third option below, because it works with the payment accounts you set up before the trip.

### 3. Mobile Payment Options (Alipay / WeChat Pay)

[Alipay](/posts/how-to-use-alipay-as-a-foreigner-complete-setup-guide-2026/) and [WeChat Pay](/posts/wechat-pay-for-foreigners-step-by-step-setup-and-common-mistakes-to-avoid-guide/) both let travelers link an international card and pay for transit with a QR code. In most cities, the same app you use for restaurants and shops also opens the metro gate -- there is no separate card or deposit.

This is the recommended default for short visits: no deposit, no extra card, and near-universal acceptance. Detailed setup steps are covered in the Alipay and WeChat Pay guides linked above.

## City Examples

### Beijing Transportation Card

Beijing's metro uses a physical IC card and official mobile QR payment options. The card can be bought at station service counters, and the Beijing metro app provides English route planning. QR payment via mobile apps is widely accepted at gates.

### Shanghai Transportation Card

Shanghai's transit system supports physical IC cards and QR payment. The Shanghai metro app includes English navigation and route planning. For short visits, mobile QR payment is the simplest way to pass the gates.

### Guangzhou / Shenzhen Transportation Card

Both cities run large metro networks that accept physical transit cards and QR payment. Shenzhen's network connects across the Pearl River Delta, making a physical card or reliable mobile payment worthwhile if you plan several city-to-city trips.

1. **Find the station service counter or ticket machine** -- every major metro station has one.
2. **Ask for a transit card** -- English service is available at major tourist stations in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Shenzhen.
3. **Pay the deposit and initial balance** -- cash and mobile payment are both commonly accepted.
4. **Top up when the balance runs low** -- machines and counters both offer top-ups.
5. **Refund the deposit before you leave** -- most cities return the deposit when the card is returned; policies vary, so confirm at the station.

If you prefer not to hold a card at all, skip this section and use QR payment via Alipay or WeChat Pay instead. New to Alipay? See our [Alipay for Foreigners guide](/posts/alipay-for-foreigners-guide/) for a step-by-step setup walkthrough.

## Which Option Is Best for Tourists?

| Trip type | Recommended option |
|---|---|
| 1-3 day city visit, phone works reliably | Alipay / WeChat Pay QR code |
| Multi-city trip (Beijing + Shanghai + Guangzhou) | Physical IC card (interconnect coverage) + QR code as backup |
| Traveling without a Chinese payment setup | Physical IC card bought with cash |
| Long stay or frequent daily metro use | Physical IC card (faster gates, refundable deposit) |

For most first-time tourists, the practical combination is: **set up Alipay or WeChat Pay before the trip, and buy a physical card only if you plan heavy metro use or multi-city travel.**

## FAQ: Transportation Cards in China

### Can I use one card in multiple Chinese cities?

Some physical IC cards join the national transit-card interconnect program, but coverage is not generally on every line. Mobile QR payment works in nearly every city without a card.

### Is there a deposit for the metro card?

Most cities require a small, refundable deposit when you buy the physical card. Return the card at a station service counter to claim it back.

### Do I need a Chinese bank account to pay for transit?

No. Alipay and WeChat Pay accept international cards for QR payment, and physical cards can be bought with cash or topped up at station machines. See our [Alipay for Foreigners guide](/posts/alipay-for-foreigners-guide/) for the full card-linking setup.

### What if my phone has no signal underground?

Station platforms in China generally have good connectivity, but if your phone fails, a physical IC card is the reliable fallback because it works offline.

### How does the transportation card compare to booking trains?

A transportation card is for local metro and bus travel inside a city. For intercity trips, you book [high-speed rail tickets](/posts/china-high-speed-rail-how-to-book-tickets/) separately -- see the [China Transportation Guide](/posts/china-transportation-complete-guide-trains-subways-taxis-and-more/) for the full comparison.
