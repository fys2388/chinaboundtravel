## Basic Greetings and Courtesies

One of the first things you'll need to learn is how to greet people in Chinese. The most common greeting is "Ni hao" (你好), which means "Hello." It's pronounced "nee-how." You can use this greeting to say hello to anyone, whether it's a stranger on the street or a friend you're meeting for the first time.

Another important greeting is "Zai jian" (再见), which means "Goodbye." It's pronounced "zigh-jyen." You can use this greeting when you're leaving someone's company or when you're saying goodbye to a friend.

In addition to greetings, it's also important to learn some basic courtesies in Chinese. For example, when you meet someone for the first time, it's polite to shake their hand and say "Ni hao" while looking them in the eye. You should also avoid standing too close to someone when you're talking to them, as this can be considered rude.

Another important courtesy in Chinese culture is to use honorifics when addressing people. For example, if you're talking to someone older than you or someone in a position of authority, you should use the title "Laoshi" (老师), which means "teacher." If you're talking to a woman, you can use the title "Nüshi" (女士), which means "lady."

## Numbers and Counting

Numbers are an essential part of any language, and Chinese is no exception. In Chinese, numbers are pronounced differently depending on whether they're being used to count things or to tell time.

To count things, you use the following numbers:
- 一 (yī) - one
- 二 (èr) - two
- 三 (sān) - three
- 四 (sì) - four
- 五 (wǔ) - five
- 六 (liù) - six
- 七 (qī) - seven
- 八 (bā) - eight
- 九 (jiǔ) - nine
- 十 (shí) - ten

To tell time, you use the following numbers:
- 一点 (yī diǎn) - one o'clock
- 两点 (liǎng diǎn) - two o'clock
- 三点 (sān diǎn) - three o'clock
- 四点 (sì diǎn) - four o'clock
- 五点 (wǔ diǎn) - five o'clock
- 六点 (liù diǎn) - six o'clock
- 七点 (qī diǎn) - seven o'clock
- 八点 (bā diǎn) - eight o'clock
- 九点 (jiǔ diǎn) - nine o'clock
- 十点 (shí diǎn) - ten o'clock

It's also important to note that in Chinese, the number "four" (sì) is considered unlucky because it sounds like the word for "death." Therefore, you should avoid using this number when counting things or when giving gifts.

## Directions and问路

Getting around in China can be a challenge, especially if you don't speak the language. That's why it's important to learn some basic directions and问路 phrases.

To ask for directions, you can use the following phrases:
- 请问，去 [地点] 怎么走？(Qǐng wèn, qù [dì diǎn] zěn me zǒu?) - Excuse me, how do I get to [place]?
- 请问，[地点] 在哪里？(Qǐng wèn, [dì diǎn] zài nǎ lǐ?) - Excuse me, where is [place]?
- 请问，附近有 [地点] 吗？(Qǐng wèn, fù jìn yǒu [dì diǎn] ma?) - Excuse me, is there a [place] nearby?

To give directions, you can use the following phrases:
- 往前走 (Wǎng qián zǒu) - Go straight ahead
- 向左转 (Xiàng zuǒ zhuǎn) - Turn left
- 向右转 (Xiàng yòu zhuǎn) - Turn right
- 过了 [地点] 就到了 (Guò le [dì diǎn] jiù dào le) - It's just past [place]
- 在 [地点] 的对面 (Zài [dì diǎn] de duì miàn) - It's opposite [place]

It's also a good idea to learn some basic landmarks and street names in the area where you're staying. This will make it easier for you to ask for directions and to find your way around.

## Food and Dining

One of the highlights of traveling in China is the food. Chinese cuisine is known for its delicious flavors, diverse ingredients, and unique cooking methods. That's why it's important to learn some basic food and dining phrases.

To order food in a restaurant, you can use the following phrases:
- 我要 [菜名] (Wǒ yào [cài míng]) - I would like [dish name]
- 请给我 [菜名] (Qǐng gěi wǒ [cài míng]) - Please give me [dish name]
- 有 [菜名] 吗？(Yǒu [cài míng] ma?) - Do you have [dish name]?
- 不要 [调料] (Bù yào [tiáo liào]) - No [seasoning]

To ask for the bill, you can use the following phrase:
- 买单 (Mǎi dān) - Check, please

It's also important to note that in Chinese culture, it's polite to share your food with others. Therefore, when you're dining with a group of people, it's a good idea to order a variety of dishes and to share them with everyone.

## Shopping and Bargaining

Shopping is a popular activity in China, and there are many markets and stores where you can buy souvenirs, clothing, electronics, and other items. That's why it's important to learn some basic shopping and bargaining phrases.

To ask for the price of an item, you can use the following phrase:
- 这个多少钱？(Zhè ge duō shǎo qián?) - How much is this?

To bargain for a lower price, you can use the following phrases:
- 太贵了 (Tài guì le) - It's too expensive
- 便宜一点 (Pián yí yì diǎn) - Can you make it cheaper?
- 打个折吧 (Dǎ ge zhé ba) - Can you give me a discount?

It's also important to note that in Chinese culture, bargaining is a common practice. Therefore, don't be afraid to negotiate the price of an item, but be polite and respectful when doing so.

## Emergency Phrases

Hopefully, you won't need to use these phrases during your trip to China, but it's always a good idea to be prepared. Here are some basic emergency phrases that you should know:
- 救命！(Jiù mìng!) - Help!
- 我迷路了。(Wǒ mí lù le.) - I'm lost.
- 我生病了。(Wǒ shēng bìng le.) - I'm sick.
- 我受伤了。(Wǒ shòu shāng le.) - I'm hurt.
- 报警！(Bào jǐng!) - Call the police!
- 叫救护车！(Jiào jiù hù chē!) - Call an ambulance!

It's also a good idea to carry a copy of your passport and other important documents with you at all times. If you lose your documents or if you need help, you can contact the nearest embassy or consulate for assistance.

## Tips for Learning Chinese Language Survival Phrases

Learning a new language can be challenging, but with a little practice and dedication, you can learn the Chinese language survival phrases that you need to get by in China. Here are some tips to help you learn:

- **Immerse yourself in the language:** Immerse yourself in the Chinese language and culture as much as possible. This will help you to learn the language more quickly and to gain a better understanding of the culture.
