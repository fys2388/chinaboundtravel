China has a diverse range of winter destinations, from the snowy landscapes of the north to the mild and sunny weather of the south. ## Best Places to Visit in China During the Winter

### Harbin: The Ice City
Harbin, the capital city of Heilongjiang province, is famous for its winter ice festivals. The city transforms into a winter wonderland, with ice sculptures and lanterns everywhere you look. The Harbin Ice and Snow World is the largest ice sculpture festival in the world, featuring towering ice castles, intricate ice carvings, and even an ice slide. It's like stepping into a scene from "Frozen"!

- **Ice and Snow World:** This is the main attraction in Harbin during the winter. The festival usually opens in early January and lasts for about two months. The ice sculptures are truly spectacular, with some reaching up to 30 meters in height. There are also many interactive activities, such as ice skating, sledding, and ice hockey.
- **Saint Sophia Cathedral:** This beautiful Orthodox church is surrounded by snow in the winter, creating a stunning backdrop for photos. The church was built in 1907 and is one of the most famous landmarks in Harbin.
- **Songhua River:** The Songhua River is a major river in China that runs through Harbin. In the winter, the river freezes over, and you can go ice skating, sledding, or take a dog sledding ride on the ice.
- **Russian Street:** Harbin has a long history of Russian influence, and there are many Russian-style buildings and streets in the city. Russian Street is one of the most popular streets in Harbin, with many restaurants, cafes, and shops.

### Beijing: The Capital City
Beijing, the capital of China, is a great destination to visit in the winter. The city is filled with historical and cultural landmarks, such as the Forbidden City, the Temple of Heaven, and the Great Wall. In the winter, these attractions take on a different look, with snow-covered roofs and a peaceful atmosphere.

- **Forbidden City:** The Forbidden City is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most famous landmarks in China. The complex was built in the 15th century and served as the imperial palace of the Ming and Qing dynasties. In the winter, the Forbidden City is covered in snow, creating a beautiful and peaceful scene.
- **Temple of Heaven:** The Temple of Heaven is another UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most important religious buildings in China. The temple was built in the 15th century and is located in the southern part of Beijing. In the winter, the Temple of Heaven is surrounded by snow, creating a beautiful and serene atmosphere.
- **Great Wall:** The Great Wall is one of the most famous landmarks in the world and a must-visit attraction in China. There are many sections of the Great Wall near Beijing, such as Badaling, Mutianyu, and Simatai. In the winter, the Great Wall is covered in snow, creating a beautiful and dramatic scene.
- **Hutongs:** The hutongs are the traditional alleyways and courtyard houses in Beijing. They are a great place to experience the local culture and way of life. In the winter, the hutongs are decorated with red lanterns and snow, creating a festive and cozy atmosphere.

### Xi'an: The Ancient Capital
Xi'an, the ancient capital of China, is another great destination to visit in the winter. The city is home to many historical and cultural landmarks, such as the Terracotta Army, the City Wall, and the Shaanxi History Museum. In the winter, these attractions are less crowded, allowing you to enjoy them at your own pace.

- **Terracotta Army:** The Terracotta Army is one of the most famous archaeological discoveries in the world and a must-visit attraction in Xi'an. The army was built in the 3rd century BC to protect the tomb of Emperor Qin Shi Huang. In the winter, the Terracotta Army is less crowded, allowing you to enjoy the exhibits at your own pace.
- **City Wall:** The City Wall of Xi'an is one of the largest and best-preserved city walls in China. The wall was built in the 14th century and is located in the center of Xi'an. In the winter, the City Wall is covered in snow, creating a beautiful and dramatic scene.
- **Shaanxi History Museum:** The Shaanxi History Museum is one of the most important museums in China and a must-visit attraction in Xi'an. The museum houses a large collection of artifacts from the history of Shaanxi province, including the Terracotta Army. In the winter, the museum is less crowded, allowing you to enjoy the exhibits at your own pace.
- **Bell Tower and Drum Tower:** The Bell Tower and Drum Tower are two of the most famous landmarks in Xi'an. The Bell Tower was built in the 14th century and is located in the center of Xi'an. The Drum Tower was built in the 13th century and is located near the Bell Tower. In the winter, the Bell Tower and Drum Tower are covered in snow, creating a beautiful and dramatic scene.

### Chengdu: The City of Pandas
Chengdu, the capital of Sichuan province, is known for its delicious food and laid-back atmosphere. In the winter, the city is relatively mild, making it a great place to visit if you don't like the cold. One of the main attractions in Chengdu is the Giant Panda Breeding Research Base. Here, you can see pandas up close and learn about their conservation efforts.

- **Giant Panda Breeding Research Base:** The Giant Panda Breeding Research Base is one of the most popular attractions in Chengdu. The base is home to many pandas, as well as other animals such as red pandas, monkeys, and birds. In the winter, the pandas are usually more active, making it a great time to visit.
- **Jinli Ancient Street:** Jinli Ancient Street is a popular tourist destination in Chengdu. The street is lined with traditional Sichuan architecture and is a great place to try some local snacks, such as hot pot, dumplings, and noodles. In the winter, the street is decorated with red lanterns and snow, creating a festive and cozy atmosphere.
- **Wuhou Temple:** Wuhou Temple is a famous temple in Chengdu. The temple was built in the 3rd century AD to commemorate the famous general Zhuge Liang. In the winter, the temple is less crowded, allowing you to enjoy the exhibits at your own pace.
- **Dujiangyan Irrigation System:** The Dujiangyan Irrigation System is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most important engineering feats in Chinese history. The system was built in the 3rd century BC and is located near Chengdu. In the winter, the system is less crowded, allowing you to enjoy the exhibits at your own pace.

- **The Bund:** The Bund is a famous waterfront area in Shanghai. The area is lined with historic buildings and is a great place to take a walk and enjoy the view. In the winter, the Bund is less crowded, allowing you to enjoy the exhibits at your own pace.

## How to Pack for a Winter Trip to China

### Clothing
When packing for a winter trip to China, it's important to bring warm clothing. The temperature can vary depending on the region, but in general, it's best to bring layers. You'll need a warm coat, hat, gloves, scarf, and insulated shoes or boots. It's also a good idea to bring some waterproof clothing, especially if you're planning to visit areas that may get snow or rain.

- **Coat:** A warm coat is essential for a winter trip to China. Look for a coat that is insulated and waterproof, such as a down coat or a parka.
- **Hat:** A hat is a great way to keep your head warm. Look for a hat that covers your ears and is made of a warm material, such as wool or fleece.
- **Gloves:** Gloves are essential for keeping your hands warm. Look for gloves that are insulated and waterproof, such as leather gloves or mittens.
- **Scarf:** A scarf is a great way to keep your neck warm. Look for a scarf that is made of a warm material, such as wool or fleece.
- **Insulated shoes or boots:** Insulated shoes or boots are essential for keeping your feet warm. Look for shoes or boots that are waterproof and have a good traction, such as hiking boots or snow boots.

- **144-hour visa-free transit:** Australian passport holders are eligible for 144-hour visa-free transit in China. This means that you can stay in China for up to 144 hours (6 days) without a visa, as long as you are traveling through China on a connecting flight or train.
- **Tourist visa:** If you plan to stay in China for longer than 144 hours, you'll need to apply for a tourist visa. The tourist visa application process can be a bit complicated, so it's best to start early and make sure you have all the required documents. ### Food
Chinese food is one of the most popular and delicious cuisines in the world. In the winter, you can try some of the local specialties, such as hot pot, dumplings, noodles, and roasted duck. Make sure you try some of the street food, as it's often the cheapest and most delicious. And don't forget to try some of the local tea, such as green tea, black tea, and oolong tea.

- **Hot pot:** Hot pot is a popular Chinese dish that is especially popular in the winter. The dish consists of a simmering pot of broth, which is usually made from beef, chicken, or pork. You can add a variety of ingredients to the broth, such as vegetables, meats, and noodles.
- **Dumplings:** Dumplings are a traditional Chinese dish that is usually filled with meat, vegetables, or a combination of both. The dumplings can be boiled, steamed, or fried, and they are often served with a dipping sauce.
- **Noodles:** Noodles are a staple of Chinese cuisine, and there are many different types of noodles to choose from. Some of the most popular types of noodles in China include wheat noodles, rice noodles, and egg noodles.
- **Roasted duck:** Roasted duck is a famous Chinese dish that is especially popular in Beijing. The duck is usually roasted until it is crispy and golden brown, and it is served with a variety of condiments, such as pancakes, scallions, and a special sauce.
- **Street food:** Street food is a popular and affordable way to try different types of Chinese food. Some of the most popular street foods in China include baozi (steamed buns), jiaozi (dumplings), roujiamo (Chinese hamburger), and tanghulu (candied fruit).
