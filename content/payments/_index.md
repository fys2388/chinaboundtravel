## Which Payment Method Should You Use?

### Use Alipay as Your Primary App

Alipay has the best English-language support and widest acceptance for foreign-issued cards. It works seamlessly for high-speed rail tickets, hotel bookings, and large purchases. The Tour Pass feature makes it easy to top up and spend without a Chinese bank account.

### Keep WeChat Pay as a Backup

WeChat Pay is essential because many smaller vendors and street food stalls prefer it. It is also the go-to for sending money to friends, splitting bills, and tipping. The setup process is similar to Alipay but the English interface is less polished.

### Always Carry Some Cash

While rare in major cities, there are still situations where cash is needed: rural areas, older establishments, and some taxi drivers. Exchange a small amount (200-500 RMB) at the airport upon arrival as your emergency fund.

## Tips for Payment Success in China

- **Set up both apps before you arrive in China.** The verification process can sometimes take time and may require internet access to Western services.
- **Screenshot your QR codes.** If your phone battery dies, having screenshots of your Alipay and WeChat Pay QR codes is a lifesaver.
- **Notify your bank before traveling.** Some foreign banks block international transactions by default for fraud prevention.
- **Use the Tour Pass.** Both Alipay and WeChat Pay offer a Tour Pass feature specifically designed for short-term visitors. It lets you load money without fully linking a bank card.

## FAQ

### Can I use my foreign credit card in China?

Yes, in 2026 both Alipay and WeChat Pay support foreign-issued Visa and Mastercard cards. However, direct card acceptance at point-of-sale terminals is still limited. Your best strategy is to link your foreign card to Alipay and WeChat Pay, then use the apps to scan QR codes for payment.

### Do I need a Chinese bank account to pay in China?

No. Both Alipay and WeChat Pay now allow foreigners to link international credit cards directly. You can also use the Tour Pass feature to top up a prepaid balance without any Chinese bank account. However, having a Chinese bank account can reduce transaction fees and raise limits.

### Is tipping expected in China?

No, tipping is not part of Chinese culture. In most restaurants and hotels, a service charge is already included. Attempting to tip may cause confusion. However, if you want to express appreciation to a tour guide or at a high-end hotel, a small tip is becoming more accepted in 2026.

### What happens if my phone dies and I cannot pay?

Always carry a small amount of cash (200-500 RMB) as a backup. Most taxi drivers, small shops, and street vendors still accept cash, though they may need to provide change. Taking a screenshot of your payment QR codes is also a good practice.

### Is China really cashless?

In major cities (Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen), it is very close to cashless. QR code payments account for over 90% of transactions. However, in smaller towns, rural areas, and with older vendors, cash is still accepted and sometimes preferred. Always carry both options.

### Can I use Apple Pay or Google Pay in China?

Apple Pay has limited acceptance through UnionPay but is not widely supported. Google Pay does not work in China due to Google services being blocked. Alipay and WeChat Pay remain the dominant payment methods for all visitors.
