## Cash Is Dead in China -- And That Surprises Every First-Timer

**By Joran | 10+ years navigating China payment systems**

Every single first-time traveler to China asks me the same question: "Do I need cash?" The answer in 2026: almost never -- if you set up Alipay correctly before you arrive.

I have not carried cash in China in over three years. Not once. I pay for street food with Alipay QR codes, buy high-speed rail tickets, book Klook tours, and split group dinners -- all from my phone, with my foreign Mastercard linked.

Here is exactly how to do it.

## The Two Apps You Need: Alipay and WeChat Pay

### Alipay -- Your Primary Payment App

Alipay is ubiquitous. Every merchant in China -- from a luxury boutique in Shanghai to a skewer stand in Xian -- accepts Alipay. It is not optional; it is the default.

**Setting up TourPASS (Foreign Card Binding):**

1. Download Alipay BEFORE arriving in China
2. Open app, tap Me (bottom right) > TourPASS
3. Upload your passport photo (required by Chinese regulations for foreign users)
4. Add your foreign card (Visa, Mastercard, or UnionPay)
5. Verify identity -- usually instant approval
6. You are ready to scan and pay

**Important:** TourPASS has a transaction limit -- initially 200 RMB/day for QR payments, higher for in-app purchases. For large purchases (hotels, flights), pay directly through the merchant app using Alipay checkout.

### WeChat Pay -- Your Backup

WeChat Pay is the second major platform. Acceptance is near-universal in major cities but drops off in tier-3 cities and rural areas.

**Setting up WeChat Pay:**
1. Open WeChat > Me > Wallet > Cards > Add Card
2. Select your card type and enter card details
3. Complete identity verification
4. Done

WeChat is also how you communicate with businesses, book Didi rides, and join group conversations -- making it arguably more essential than Alipay for daily life in China.

## The Honest Problems (And Solutions)

### Problem 1: My Card Keeps Declining
**Cause:** Most Western banks block China transactions by default for fraud protection.
**Fix:** Call your bank 48 hours before departure. Tell them you are traveling to China and need to enable POS and online transactions.

### Problem 2: I Cannot Get Change Back
**Cause:** Some small vendors do not carry much change.
**Fix:** Use Alipay for exact amounts. Carry 100 RMB bills rather than larger denominations.

### Problem 3: The Vendor Says "WeChat Only" or "Alipay Only"
**Reality:** In 2026, about 80% of merchants accept both. Just use whichever they display.

### Problem 4: My Phone Battery Dies
**Solution:** Carry a portable charger (10,000mAh minimum). In an emergency, you CAN pay with a physical UnionPay card at large supermarkets, but most small vendors do not accept cards.

## The One Thing No One Tells You: Alipay Has a Built-In Exchange Rate

When you use Alipay with a foreign card, it automatically converts currencies at the mid-market rate. This is actually better than most airport exchange kiosks.

The key is: always pay in the local currency (RMB/CNY). Never let a vendor charge you in your home currency -- that "convenience" comes with a 3-5% hidden markup.
