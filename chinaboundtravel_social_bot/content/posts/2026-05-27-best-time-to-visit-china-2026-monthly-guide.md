## China Does not Have One Travel Season — It Has Six Climates

**By Joran | 10+ years of badly-timed trips and lessons learned**

I have been to the Great Wall in August (suffocating), January (freezing and magical), and October during Golden Week (never again). China geographic scale means "when to go" depends entirely on WHERE you are going.

This guide covers each region ideal timing so you can plan smarter.

## Month-by-Month Breakdown

### January — Cold, Cheap, Avoid CNY Week
**Best for:** Beijing winter photography, Harbin Ice Festival, Guangzhou escape
**Avoid:** First 2 weeks (Chinese New Year chaos), Xinjiang, Tibet

Beijing in January is stunning — Forbidden City covered in snow is a bucket-list experience. Harbin transforms into a crystal ice palace. Flights are cheapest mid-January. Pack thermal layers, down jacket, hand warmers.

### February — Chinese New Year (Dates Vary)
**Best for:** Experiencing CNY festivities if you are culturally curious
**Avoid:** Everything else — the world busiest domestic travel period

CNY falls between January 21 and February 20. The week before and after are chaos. But if you are IN China during CNY — the fireworks, temple fairs, and dumplings are unforgettable.

### March — Cherry Blossoms and Rebirth
**Best for:** Nanjing, Wuhan, Beijing, Shanghai late month
**Beijing:** 5-15C, increasingly pleasant

One of China most most beautiful months. Cherry blossoms in Nanjing Purple Mountain and Beijing gardens. Weather is warming but unpredictable — pack layers.

### April — Peak Shoulder Season
**Best for:** Everywhere. Seriously.
**Beijing:** 10-22C, mostly sunny
**Shanghai:** 13-22C, pleasant

April is the Goldilocks month. Clear skies, comfortable temperatures, flowers blooming, crowds manageable outside weekends. This is when I recommend most travelers come. Book your Great Wall trip early — April weekends at Mutianyu fill up.

### May — Still Good, But Watch Labor Day Week
**Best for:** Western Sichuan (green and accessible), Yunnan, Zhangjiajie
**Avoid:** First week of May (Labor Day domestic travel surge)

May in Western Sichuan: grasslands turning green, wildflowers blooming, weather stable. One warning: May 1 is a 3-day holiday. Domestic travel spikes. Book ahead.

### June-August — Summer Peak
**Best for:** Coastal cities, high altitude areas, northern cities
**Avoid:** South China intensive outdoor sightseeing

Heat and humidity makes city sightseeing in Guangzhou, Shenzhen, and Shanghai miserable by afternoon. Western Sichuan stays comfortable. Qingdao and Dalian are pleasant coastal escapes.

### September-October — Second Shoulder Season
**Best for:** Everywhere, especially autumn foliage
**Avoid:** October 1-7 (Golden Week — book 3 months ahead or avoid)

September is spectacular — autumn colors in Beijing Fragrant Hills, clear skies, comfortable temperatures. October 1-7 is Golden Week: over 600 million people travel. Avoid it or plan accordingly.

### November-December — Winter Pricing
**Best for:** Beijing winter photography, cheap flights, fewer tourists
**Avoid:** Southern China (gray and damp)

Flights from the US drop significantly November-December. Beijing is photogenic and uncrowded. Harbin ice festival starts December.

## My Personal Recommendation

**Come in late April or early October.** Book your flights 4-6 months out to lock in shoulder-season pricing. Avoid Golden Week by at least 5 days on either side.

And whatever month you come — pack layers. China indoor heating and outdoor weather in the same day can vary by 20C.
