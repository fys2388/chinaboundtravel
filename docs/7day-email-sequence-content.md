**Preview text:** Visa-free entry rules, eligibility & border tips

**Body:**

Hi again,

Let's tackle the #1 question every traveler asks: **Do I need a visa?**

### The short answer

It depends on your passport. As of 2026, China has expanded visa-free entry to cover travelers from **many countries** for stays of 15, 30, or even 90 days.

👉 **[Check if your country qualifies for visa-free entry]**

### If you need a visa

- Apply at least **4 weeks** before departure
- Standard tourist visa (L-type) is usually valid for 10 years (US passport) or 30-90 days
- Processing time: 3-5 business days in most countries

### Border crossing tips

1. **Fill out the arrival card** on the plane — saves time at immigration
2. **Have your hotel address ready** — officers may ask where you're staying
3. **Keep your passport valid for 6+ months** beyond your departure date
4. **Declare cash over $5,000** — not doing so can cause delays

### Travel insurance for China

While you're sorting documents, don't skip travel insurance. China's medical costs for foreigners can be high, and travel insurance covers trip cancellations, lost luggage, and emergencies.

## Day 3: Payments & Money

**Subject:** Day 3: How to pay in China (without a Chinese bank)

**Preview text:** Alipay, WeChat Pay, cash & what to budget

**Body:**

Hi,

China is essentially a **cashless society** — even street food vendors accept QR code payments. Here's how to set up before you go.

### Mobile payment setup

**Alipay** (most widely accepted):
1. Download the Alipay app
2. Sign up with your passport
3. Link your international credit card (Visa/Mastercard)
4. Use "Tour Card" for prepaid top-ups

**WeChat Pay**:
1. Download WeChat
2. Go to Me → Wallet → Cards
3. Add your international card
4. Works at most places that accept Alipay

### Cash is still useful

Carry **500-1000 yuan** in cash for:
- Small vendors that don't accept mobile payments
- Taxis in smaller cities
- Temple donations
- Emergency situations

### Daily budget guide

## Day 5: Transportation

**Subject:** Day 5: Getting around China — trains, subways & taxis

**Preview text:** High-speed rail booking shortcuts + city transport tips

**Body:**

Hi,

China has one of the best transportation systems in the world — once you know how to use it.

### High-speed rail (the star of the show)

China's high-speed rail network is **the largest on Earth**, with trains reaching 350 km/h (217 mph).

**Booking tips:**
- Book through **Trip.com** or the official **12306** app
- Tickets sell out fast — book **2-4 weeks ahead** for popular routes
- Choose **second class** (二等座) — comfortable and great value
- Arrive 30-40 minutes early — stations are huge

**Popular routes:**
- Beijing → Xi'an: 4.5 hours
- Beijing → Shanghai: 4.5-6 hours
- Shanghai → Hangzhou: 45 minutes
- Chengdu → Chongqing: 1-1.5 hours

→ **[Book train tickets on Trip.com]**

### City transport

**Subway**: Every major city has an extensive subway system. Buy a transit card at any station, or use Alipay/WeChat to scan QR codes at the gates.

**Didi** (China's Uber): Download the Didi app, link your payment method, and you're set. Cheaper than taxis and available everywhere.

**Taxis**: Metered in most cities. Have your destination written in Chinese (use Pleco or Google Translate) to avoid confusion.

### Airport transport

- **Shanghai Pudong**: Maglev train (8 min to city) or Metro Line 2
- **Beijing Capital**: Airport Express subway (25 min)
- **Guangzhou Baiyun**: Metro Line 3
- Most airports also have airport buses (cheaper, slower)

### Xi'an

**Best area: City Center / near the Bell Tower**
- Inside the ancient city walls
- Walking distance to Muslim Quarter, Bell Tower, Drum Tower
- Great base for exploring the city

**Note:** Terracotta Army is 1.5 hours outside the city — no need to stay near it. Do it as a day trip.

→ **[Search hotels in Xi'an]**

### Shanghai

**Best area: The Bund / Nanjing Road**
- Iconic location with skyline views
- Walking distance to The Bund, Yu Garden, Nanjing Road shopping
- Excellent subway hub

**Alternative: French Concession**
- Tree-lined streets, cafes, boutiques
- Trendy and walkable
- Farther from major tourist sites but great for atmosphere

→ **[Search hotels in Shanghai]**

### Booking tips

1. **Book in advance** — popular hotels sell out, especially during holidays
2. **Check the location on a map** — "near subway" can mean a 15-minute walk
3. **Read recent reviews** — Chinese hotel standards vary widely
4. **International vs local chains** — International chains (Marriott, Hilton) are reliable; local chains (Hanting, Home Inn) are budget-friendly but basic
